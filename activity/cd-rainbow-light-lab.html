CD Rainbow Light Lab

One old CD, one flashlight, and a white wall — split plain light into a rainbow and bend it around.

Ages
6–12
Group
2–30 kids
Time
15–25 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Dim the room if you can — rainbows show best against shade. Check each CD for cracked or sharp edges and retire those.

Materials

  • an old CD or DVD (one per pair)
  • a flashlight
  • white paper or a pale wall
  • a sunny window (optional)

Steps

  1. Shine the flashlight at the CD's shiny side at an angle. Hunt slowly until a rainbow lands on the paper.
  2. Tilt and turn: the rainbow stretches, splits, and slides. Each pair finds the widest rainbow they can make and holds it still for a count of ten.
  3. Try the sunny window next (CD in the light, rainbow on paper — eyes never toward the sun). Compare: which light gives the stronger rainbow?
  4. Draw what you saw: the order of colors, every time, is the same. Red on one edge, violet on the other.
  5. The honest explanation: the CD's tiny grooves act like a fence that sorts light by color. Sorted light is a rainbow.

Variations

Easier: Work in pairs with the adult holding the CD steady; the hunt is the job.

Harder: Test other grooved or shiny surfaces (a phone screen off, a vinyl record) and rank which sorts light best.

⚠️ Safety

Never look at the sun or aim a reflection at anyone's eyes. Retire cracked CDs — the edges are sharp.
