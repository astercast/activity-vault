Giant Steps, Baby Steps

The caller names a step, a GIANT step, a baby step, a marching step, and the whole group takes it together, all the way to the garden gate.

Ages 3–3 · 10–15 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–3
Group
4–16 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Mark the two lines yourself and walk the distance at a baby step before the group lines up: the whole trip should take a mixed bag of calls, eight to twelve steps in total, because a journey of thirty calls loses its travellers and a journey of three never becomes one. Clear the lane as you walk it: at three, looking at your own feet is the game, so the floor owes the group a clear view and nothing to trip over.

Materials

  • a start line and a finish line (tape, a rug edge, the garden gate itself)
  • no other materials, legs only

Steps

  1. Teach the steps standing on the start line, before any travelling: a GIANT step is the biggest stretch a leg can honestly make; a baby step is heel-to-toe tiny, a foot-length and no more; a marching step is knees up, in the middle, and proud. The group practises all three on the line until the sizes are in their legs. Sizes are the vocabulary of this game and the travelling makes no sense without them.
  2. State the one rule, whole: 'The caller calls a step. EVERYBODY takes that step together. Nobody is ever out, nobody is sent back, and we all reach the gate.' That last part is the design, said out loud at the start so the group hears the promise the game keeps.
  3. Travelling round: you call, 'GIANT step!', and the whole line stretches one giant step together and freezes in the stretch a moment, marvelling at the distance; 'Baby step, baby step', two tiny ones, heel to toe; 'Marching steps, one, two, three!' The calls vary the sizes on purpose: after two giants, a run of babies makes the gate feel far again, and the group will groan happily, because the distance changing size IS the game.
  4. Last-step ceremony: when the group is one call from the gate, stop and look at the gap together and let the group choose the final step by vote of voices. It will be a giant, and it should be: the whole line takes it together and lands at the gate in one stretched, laughing row, exactly as promised at the start line.
  5. Finish at the gate with the journey told back in sizes: 'Two giants got us started. Then the babies made us wait. Then we MARCHED.' Kids will re-walk the journey in the telling, feet remembering the sizes without a call. Then the whole group turns round, because the gate is also a start line, and the way back is the same game in the other direction, if legs are willing.

Variations

Easier: Three calls only over a short lane, the adult walking each call INSIDE the line with the group, taking the same size step. A model in the middle is worth ten from the front.

Harder: New sizes join the vocabulary: a twirling step (one slow turn as you travel), a sideways crab step, a tiptoe step, each taught on the line first, then callable, and inventors of a step get it called by their name once: 'A ___ step!'

⚠️ Safety

Everybody moves on every call and nobody is ever sent back or out. That is not a softening of this game, it is the game, and a caller who suggests sending someone back is gently reminded of the promise made on the start line. Giant steps are stretches, not leaps: feet stay in contact with the ground's story, one foot down before the other leaves, because a line of leaping three-year-olds lands on each other. The lane is adult-cleared, the group travels in a wide line rather than a bunch, and a fallen traveller stops the calls until they are up and brushed off, the gate waits, it always waits.

🤝 Inclusion notes

A step is a step at every size it honestly comes in: a wheel's roll of one big push is a giant step, a short roll is a baby step, and the caller calls the same sizes to the whole line, wheels and feet travelling in one row, arriving in one row. A traveller can also be the caller for a stretch: calling the sizes IS playing the game at full stretch, and a caller who watches the line take their call learns the sizes by eye the way the line learns them by leg.
