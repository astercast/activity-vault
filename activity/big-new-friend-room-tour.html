Big New Friend Room Tour

A new friend needs three places first: where the cups live, where the coats hang, and where the quiet is. The group shows all three, pointing and naming.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–14 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
No prep needed

Setup

Decide the tour's three places before the group gathers, and decide them the way a three-year-old's first day actually runs: where my cup lives (and whose hands may fetch it), where my coat hangs (and which hook could be mine), and where the quiet is (the corner a person goes to when the room is too much, shown with the same pride as the other two, because knowing where the quiet is, is belonging). Walk the route yourself at child height and child pace: it should take about forty small steps with no squeezing past furniture, no doors the adult must fight, and nothing on the way that a pointing hand could knock into a face. If a real new child is the guest, brief nobody and rehearse nothing: the tour is offered, and watching the whole thing from a safe lap counts as taking it. That rule is set before the guest arrives, not negotiated after.

Materials

  • the group's own room, as it stands (the tour's whole territory, its cup shelf, its coat hooks, its quiet corner, in the room's ordinary state, not tidied into a show home)
  • one soft friend to be the guest when no real new child is visiting (the class bear or another famous soft resident, carried by a rotating carrier, greeted by name, treated exactly as a guest is treated)

Steps

  1. Gather the group by the door, the room's threshold, where every arrival stands, and say the plain framing: 'When somebody is new in a room, everything in it is a question. Where does my cup go? Where does my coat hang? Where is the quiet, if I need it? Today we practise answering, the way this room answers: by showing. Our guest, ' (the guest is named: the real new child, welcomed at full size, or the bear, presented with identical ceremony) ', will be shown three places by the people who live here. You.' State the laws whole: 'Tours walk. Guides point and name. Hands stay kind and off other people's things: we point AT the cup shelf, we do not rearrange it. The guest may look as long as looking takes. Nobody is quizzed at the end: showing is the whole job, and it ends when the showing is done.'
  2. First place together, the cups: the whole tour walks the forty steps at the guest's pace, which is set by the slowest walker without comment, and gathers where the guest can see the shelf properly. Two guides, chosen by list, do the showing in the session's official shape: one points, one names: 'THIS is where the cups live. Yours would go HERE, ' (the pointing hand shows the honest spare space, not a space invented for the day) ', and the water is THERE, and an adult helps, always.' If the guest is real and reaches for a cup, the reach is honoured as the tour working, and the adult quietly makes the cup theirs: naming a cup is lovely, but handing it over is the room meaning it.
  3. Second place, the coats, and third, the quiet: the route continues at the same unhurried pace, with the pointing-and-naming passing to fresh guides at each stop so the honours spread around the room, at the hooks: 'Coats hang HERE. This hook is spare. It could be yours in one minute, if you wanted it to be', and at the quiet corner, the tour's most important stop, the guides say the record's plainest line at full size: 'And THIS is where the quiet is. Anybody may come here. You do not have to ask, and nobody follows you in. It is part of the room, like the cups.' The guest is shown how the corner works, its cushion, its soft thing, its boundary, by a guide sitting in it for three breaths, demonstrating that the corner is used by the room's own people, not reserved for its saddest ones.
  4. Guest's-choice stretch, the tour's heart: the route walked, the guest chooses the place they liked best, and the whole tour goes back to it together. A teddy guest's choice is made by its carrier, at the carrier's own honest preference, and a real guest's choice can also be the door, the window, or nowhere yet, and every one of those is received as a complete answer: 'The door is a good favourite. It is where the room begins.' At the chosen place the group settles for one minute of simply being there together, drinking the pretend tea of arrival, saying nothing that needs saying, because a tour that ends by USING the room together teaches more than the pointing did.
  5. Finish back at the threshold, where tours end: the guest is thanked for visiting ('You let us show you our room. Showing it made it more ours, not less') and the guides are thanked by name for the specific thing each one showed. 'You showed where the quiet is. A new person will remember that longest.' The tell-back is said at the door: 'Three places: where the cups live, where the coats hang, and where the quiet is. Shown by the people who live here, at walking pace, with pointing hands. Whoever arrives in this room next gets this tour, because now this room knows how to give it.' The bear, if it was the guest, keeps a place of honour by the door for the week: the room's greeter, on duty.

Variations

Easier: A one-place version: only the quiet corner is shown, with full ceremony, the whole record inside ten minutes, for a day when one place is what a real arrival needs.

Harder: A photograph version (a real arrival expected next week): the guides pose at each of the three places, the adult photographs the pointing and the naming, and the three pictures wait by the door for the guest who has not come yet, a tour that travels ahead of its guest.

Safety

The tour stays inside the group's own room, on the route the adult walked at child height first: no toilets, no staff areas, no other rooms, and no door in the route is opened by a child's hands. Doors on the way are the adult's, held, every time. Pointing hands point at things, never into faces, and other children's belongings on the route, the cups and coats of the room's own people, are looked at and named, never handled, rearranged, or tried on: a tour that disturbs the room teaches the opposite of welcome, and the adult restores anything touched without comment and without blame. A real guest child is never the tour's exhibit: they walk it, watch it from a lap, or leave it halfway, all at full honour, and the group's excitement is coached in the framing. A new person is shown the room, not surrounded by it.

Inclusion notes

Guiding divides cleanly into jobs at every distance: the pointer points, the namer names, the carrier carries the guest, the pace-setter walks first and sets the guest's pace, and a guide who travels the room differently still points from their own position, because the three places can be shown from a seat at each stop as truly as from a stance beside them. Naming arrives in any register the room owns: spoken, signed, tapped on the place itself, or given by the guide holding up the room's picture of the place while the adult speaks the name the guide has chosen. The credit for the showing stays the guide's in the tell-back, by name. The guest's honours are sized to the guest: a real new child who says nothing all tour and then chooses the quiet corner has taken the tour at its deepest, and the record receives it that way.
