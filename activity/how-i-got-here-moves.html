How I Got Here Moves

Tell the group how you travel — feet, wheels, bus, car — and everyone copies the movement: marching feet, steering hands, a gentle bus bounce.

Ages
3–3
Group
6–20 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

No set-up beyond a circle with room to move in place. Think about your own group before you start: you need the travel kinds your kids actually use — walking, car, bus, bike seat, stroller, wheelchair — and every single one gets a movement the whole group can copy with respect and pleasure. Your own travel goes first, told honestly, so the telling is modelled before anyone small is asked to do it.

Materials

  • no materials — voices and bodies only

Steps

  1. Model the whole turn with your own journey: 'I got here in a car. I steered like this' — hands on an invisible wheel, gentle turns — 'Now everybody drive my car with me.' The whole group steers together for a few beats. That copying is the welcome: your way of arriving just got driven by the entire room.
  2. State the one rule: 'One teller at a time, and we copy EVERY way of getting here the same — because every way got somebody here.' Go in seat order, known in advance, so nobody wonders when their turn is coming or whether it is.
  3. Tellers' round: each kid names their travel — in words, by pointing at themselves and miming it, or by choosing between two the adult offers out loud — then shows the movement: marching feet for walking, a bounce in the seat for the bus, pedalling legs for a bike, smooth rolling hands for wheels. The group copies it together, properly, for a real few seconds.
  4. The copying is the point and it is never a joke at the teller's expense: movements are copied the way the teller showed them, at the teller's size. If a movement is big, the room does it big; if it is small and careful, the room does it small. Matching a friend exactly is the friendship being practised.
  5. Finish with the whole journey home: you call the group's travels back in order — 'First we marched with ___, then we steered with ___' — and the room replays every movement once, fast, like a film of the morning. Every way of getting here is in the film, because every kid in the room is.

Variations

Easier: Adult offers two choices to each teller — 'Did your feet bring you, or did wheels?' — and a point is a complete answer that earns the group's full copy.

Harder: Add the middle of the journey: 'What did you see on the way?' — one thing per teller, named or mimed, and the group copies the looking as well as the travelling.

⚠️ Safety

Movements are copied kindly and exactly — never bigger-funny, never with sound effects at a teller's expense; the rule is stated once, plainly, before the first turn. All movement happens in place in the circle: marching feet stay on one spot, because twenty three-year-olds actually marching is a parade leaving the activity behind. A teller's real travel is never corrected — if they say the bus and you know it was a car, the bus is what the room copies; this is their telling, not a transport audit.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Every way of arriving is already in the room: wheels are a first-class movement here, shown by the kid who travels that way and copied by everyone, exactly as shown — the teller is the group's expert on their own journey. A teller can show their movement with one hand, with their head, or by choosing the adult's demonstration of it and nodding it correct — directing your own movement's copy is telling in full.
