Rangoli Pattern Art

Dot grid, chalk colors, mirror the pattern, floor art that welcomes people in, Diwali-style.

Ages 5–12 · 25–40 min · 2 materials

Ages
5–12
Group
2–30 kids
Time
25–40 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Draw one small dot grid yourself to demo on. Outdoors needs a swept patch of pavement; indoors, dark paper stands in for the doorstep.

Materials

  • colored chalk (pavement) or colored paper dots and dark paper (indoor version)
  • a ruler or dot-grid drawn in pencil

Steps

  1. Say what rangoli is, plainly: during Diwali, families make bright patterns at their doorways to welcome guests and good fortune. Ours follows the same idea. A welcome you can see from the door.
  2. Start every design the rangoli way: a grid of dots. Count them out loud. The grid is the skeleton.
  3. Connect and mirror: draw a petal or diamond in one corner of the grid, then repeat it in all four, turned each time. Symmetry does the heavy lifting.
  4. Color it in chalk or paper dots, brightest in the center.
  5. Leave the finished patterns by the classroom door for a day. A welcome mat you made yourself.

Variations

Easier: Use a 3x3 dot grid and one shape repeated, with the adult drawing the grid.

Harder: Design on a 7x7 grid with two mirrored shapes, or work out the pattern on paper first and scale it up to pavement size.

⚠️ Safety

Chalk stays on pavement and paper. Wash hands after. Chalk dust and snacks do not mix.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Rangoli is a living Diwali tradition, not a generic pattern exercise. Name the holiday, the doorway, and the welcome. Make patterns, not pictures of deities, and keep the focus on the craft families actually practice.
