Two Foot Landing Pads

Foam pads on the floor and one job: jump with two feet, land with two feet, and stick it for a count of three.

Ages 3–5 · 10–20 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–5
Group
2–14 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Lay the pads a small hop apart, one short two-foot jump for a three-year-old leg, tested with your own smallest hop, not your comfortable one, and press each pad flat: a pad that slides when you push it with your foot does not go down; on hard floors, carpet squares with grippy backs or pads on a big rug are the honest setup.

Materials

  • flat foam mats, carpet squares, or big rubber pads (4-6, non-slip)
  • floor or grass space

Steps

  1. Teach the landing before any jumping, standing still: feet apart, knees bent a little, arms out. 'Land like a cat, soft and stuck'. Practise the shape on the spot: small bend, hold, and the group counts one-two-three out loud while everyone holds it.
  2. First hops are step-offs, not jumps: stand on one pad, step off with two feet onto the floor and land in the cat shape, held for the count. Stepping down teaches the landing with nothing to be brave about.
  3. Pad to pad: one kid on the course at a time, two-foot jump, two-foot landing, hold for the group's count of three before the next hop. The count is done by the watchers. Holding a landing while your friends count is the game, and skipping the hold is noticed kindly and redone.
  4. A stuck landing earns the arms-up finish: after the count, arms rise and the pad is 'claimed'. A landing that travels, a step, a hop-forward, hands down, is just information: 'That one rolled. Again?' and the kid tries the same hop once more.
  5. Finish by gathering the pads into one big landing island and taking the final small jump onto it together, one at a time, each landing held while the whole group counts to three, then pads stacked by the course crew, two hands per pad.

Variations

Easier: Pads touching each other, so the 'jump' is a two-foot step across a seam, landing shape only, no air at all.

Harder: One pad a little further, or the landing held for a count of five. Longer hold, same hop.

⚠️ Safety

Flat non-slip pads only, spaced for a small hop, never for a leap, and only one kid jumps at a time, with the landing pad clear before they go. If a pad slides in testing, it does not go down; a moving pad is a fall wearing a game costume.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Stepping across counts as a crossing in full: a kid who moves pad to pad by stepping, or wheels across the pads laid as a flat path, is doing the course. The landing hold becomes a stop-and-hold at each pad, counted by the group exactly the same way. The skill is stopping on purpose, and every body in the room can practise that.
