Ofrenda Memory Circle

Build a small class ofrenda and share happy memories of people we miss.

Ages
7–12
Group
4–30 kids
Time
30–45 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Set up the table with the cloth. Arrange marigolds and LED candles. Explain first: Día de los Muertos is a Mexican celebration where families honor loved ones who have died — building ofrendas with photos, marigolds, food, and candles to welcome their memory back. We're learning about it with respect.

Materials

  • a small table and cloth
  • paper marigolds (or drawn ones)
  • LED candles only
  • printed photos (optional — kids can also just bring a memory)
  • paper and pencils

Steps

  1. Each kid writes or draws a happy memory of someone they miss — a grandparent, a pet, anyone.
  2. One at a time, kids may place their memory on the ofrenda and share it aloud — or just place it quietly. Passing is always okay.
  3. Light the LED candles together as a symbol of remembrance.
  4. Sit in a circle and share: what is one thing you learned from the person you remembered?
  5. Leave the ofrenda up through the week. Take memories home on Friday.

Variations

Easier: The teacher builds the ofrenda and kids simply add drawings — no sharing aloud required.

Harder: Research real ofrenda elements (pan de muerto, sugar skulls, copal) and add labeled paper versions.

⚠️ Safety

LED candles only — no real flames, ever. Sharing is always optional; some kids may prefer to listen.
