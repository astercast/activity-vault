Jump the River Ropes

Two ropes on the ground make a river. Stand at the bank, jump with two feet, land on the other side.

Ages 3–5 · 10–20 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–5
Group
2–16 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Lay the two ropes parallel, about a foot apart. A three-year-old's honest two-foot jump, tested against the smallest jumper you know, not the biggest. Anchor the ends so the ropes lie still; a riverbank that slides when stepped on ends the game early. Put the landing on carpet, mats, or grass.

Materials

  • two ropes, tape lines, or long scarves (the riverbanks)
  • a mat or grass for the landing side if the floor is hard

Steps

  1. Stand at the bank yourself and show the whole jump slowly: feet together, knees bend, swing arms, jump, land on two feet and freeze like a statue for one count. Landing still is part of the jump. Say so, and show the wobble too.
  2. One jumper at a time. Everyone else stands on the far bank as the welcoming side. The cheer when a jumper lands is half the equipment of this game.
  3. A jumper who steps in the river has not failed anything: the current got them, they climb out, and they jump again from the bank. Rivers at three are for practising on.
  4. When the whole group has crossed twice, move the far rope out by one hand-width. One hand-width is the correct amount of harder, and the group should watch you measure it.
  5. Last crossings are dealer's choice: jump it, or take the stepping-stone version, one big step across, and the group finishes by rolling the river up together.

Variations

Easier: Narrow the river to a hand-width, or drop the jump entirely: a big step across a tape line is the same game at an earlier page.

Harder: Widen the river another hand-width, or cross it backwards. Turn around on the bank, jump, and land facing where you came from.

⚠️ Safety

Ropes lie flat and still, the landing is soft, and only one kid jumps at a time with the landing clear before the next jump starts. Bare feet or grippy shoes. Socks on hard floors turn landings into slides.
