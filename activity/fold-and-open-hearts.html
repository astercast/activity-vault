Fold-and-Open Symmetry Hearts

Fold, draw half a heart on the fold, cut, open. The fold did the matching. Symmetry you can hold up to the light.

Before you begin

Ages
4–8
Group
2–28 kids
Time
15–25 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Fold and cut one heart yourself first, and keep the folded half, the before, next to the opened heart. That pair of papers teaches the whole lesson before you say a word.

Materials

  • colored paper (reds and pinks, plus any color a kid insists on)
  • blunt scissors
  • pencils
  • string and tape (for a garland, optional)

Steps

  1. Show the trick plainly: folded paper, a half-heart drawn along the fold, one cut, opened up, a whole heart with both sides matching. 'The fold did the matching' is the sentence of the day.
  2. Kids fold their own paper in half, crease it hard with a fingernail, and draw half a heart against the fold: bump at the top, point at the bottom.
  3. Cut on the line, an adult cuts for anyone whose scissors are still new, then open slowly and check the match: hold it to the light, fold it back shut, do the two halves sit on each other?
  4. Make three more and sort them: biggest, smallest, best match. A lopsided heart gets talked through out loud. Was the drawing off the fold, or the cut?, and remade. Paper is cheap, and the fix is the learning.
  5. String the best-matching hearts into a garland for the window, fold lines vertical, so the symmetry is the decoration.

Variations

Easier: The adult pre-draws the half-heart on the fold; the kid only cuts and opens.

Harder: Cut a small pattern into the folded edge, notches, a tiny window, and predict what the opened heart will show before opening it.

Safety

Blunt scissors and seated cutting; an adult does the cutting for any kid who asks or whose hands aren't there yet. Nobody bleeds for a valentine.
