Show Me Three Fingers

Everybody in this room is three — so everybody learns the hard, proud trick of holding up three fingers, and the whole circle copies yours.

Ages
3–3
Group
4–18 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

No set-up beyond knowing your room: this is the three-year-old room's own party trick, and it only works because everyone in it shares the number. Practise your own three first with the hand you will model with — thumb holding the little finger down is the classic route, but fingers that arrive at three by any honest road are the point of the session, and your model should be the achievable kind, wobble included.

Materials

  • no materials — hands only, ten fingers per expert

Steps

  1. Hold your hand up and say the plain boast that starts it: 'I am going to tell you something about this whole room. Every single person here is THREE. And three has a sign. Watch.' Make your three slowly — folding the little finger down, holding it with your thumb — and count the standing fingers out loud: 'One, two, THREE. That is us.'
  2. State the one rule: 'One shower at a time, and the whole circle copies their three — exactly their three, wobbles and all.' Go in seat order, known in advance, so every shower knows their moment is coming and nobody's hand is a surprise.
  3. Showers' round: each kid raises their three any way their hand can manage it — the classic fold, fingers spread wide, a three built with the other hand's help, a three held for one second before the fingers spring back. The circle copies it faithfully: spread for spread, wobble for wobble, and counts the copy together — 'One, two, THREE' — because the count is the room agreeing the sign is real.
  4. Hard-finger honesty: somewhere in the round, a little finger will refuse to stay down, and it gets named kindly as the room's shared enemy: 'That little finger has its own ideas. Everybody's does.' A hand helped by its partner hand is not a failed three — it is the standard three at this age, and the circle copying the helped version says so without a speech.
  5. Finish with the whole room's sign at once: 'Show me three!' — every hand up, every version of three the room owns, held for a count of three, counted by everyone. Photograph it in your memory if not in fact: eighteen different threes, all correct, is the three-year-old room's group portrait.

Variations

Easier: Two fingers first as a warm-up sign ('show me two — the easy cousin'), then three: the room feels its own hands level up inside one session.

Harder: Signs grow: thumbs-up for 'yes', a wave for 'hello', a flat palm for 'stop' — the circle builds its first three-sign language, every sign copied the shower's way.

⚠️ Safety

Hands only, at hand height: a showing hand never goes in another kid's face, and copies are made looking at your own hand, not grabbing a neighbour's fingers to arrange them — fingers get folded by their owner or their owner's other hand, nobody else's. A hand that does not want to show rests, and its owner directs a copy from the adult's hand instead; the circle copies that one with the same respect, because directing your sign is showing it.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Three arrives by every road: folded with a thumb, held down by the other hand, spread wide and counted, or shown on a hand resting on a tray with fingers lifted one by one while the circle counts them standing. A shower who directs the adult's hand — 'that one down... that one up... THREE' — has built the sign in full, and the circle copies the directed three exactly as proudly as any other.
