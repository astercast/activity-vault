Big Magnet Wand: It Sticks

One big magnet wand and a tray of big things: touch each one and find out — it sticks, or it doesn't. Your hand is the lab.

Ages
3–3
Group
2–10 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Check every test object with your own wand before the group sits down: the metal lids must actually stick (some lids are aluminium and will not — an all-fails tray teaches the wrong lesson), and every rim gets a thumb run around it for sharpness; a lid that fails either check does not reach the table. Set each place with a mixed handful — some that stick, some that don't — because a tray that all sticks is a demo, not a test.

Materials

  • big encased magnet wands (one per kid — the chunky classroom kind, magnet sealed inside)
  • big smooth metal jar lids and lids from big cans (adult-checked, no sharp rims)
  • big non-metal things to test (a wooden block, a big plastic lid, a sponge, a fabric scrap)
  • two trays per kid (sticks / doesn't)

Steps

  1. Start with the wand alone in the air: wave it, turn it over, look at it together. Say the one plain fact that frames everything: 'This wand is a magnet. Magnets stick to SOME things. Our job is finding out which.' No more theory than that — the table is the theory now.
  2. State the one rule: 'One thing at a time. Touch it with the wand. If it sticks, it rides to the sticks tray. If it doesn't, it goes to the other tray.' First test together, slowly: touch a big lid, lift — it comes with the wand, and the whole group says it: 'It sticks!'
  3. Testing round: each kid works through their handful at their own pace, touch, lift or not, sort, with the two sentences running constantly — 'It sticks!' / 'It doesn't.' The sentences are the data at three, and the trays filling up are the graph.
  4. Surprise check, halfway: hold up the two trays and look together. The sticks tray is all lids; the other tray is wood, plastic, sponge, cloth. Say the pattern in the plainest words: 'The sticking ones are all metal lids. The magnet is choosing the metal.' That sentence is earned by their own hands by now.
  5. Finish with the wand trick that never fails: a lid stuck UNDER the table, held through it, sliding along as the wand moves on top — do it once yourself, then let each tester try while the group watches the lid travel by itself. Trays tipped back into the basket by their testers; wands collected and counted.

Variations

Easier: Four objects only, two that stick and two that don't, and the adult does the lifting while the kid points at the right tray — choosing the tray is the test result.

Harder: Hide-and-stick round: the adult hides three lids under a cloth and the tester fishes with the wand alone — if it comes up stuck to the wand, it was metal, sight unseen.

⚠️ Safety

Encased classroom wands only — the magnet is sealed inside the plastic and can never come out. Loose small magnets of any kind never appear at this table, ever: they are a genuine emergency if swallowed, and this record says so in plain words so no substitute improvises with fridge magnets. Lids are big, smooth-rimmed and adult-checked; wand work stays on the table, and wands are never put in mouths.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Sorting is a full test done by direction: a kid who points at a tray while a partner lifts has called the result, and calling it is the science. The wand can be worked two-handed, rested on the table edge, or moved along a lap tray — the tray set-up travels to the tester, never the other way round.
