Dandelion Clock Blow Walk

A slow walk to find a ripe dandelion clock: hold it low, blow, and count your puffs while the seeds float away like tiny parachutes.

Ages
3–3
Group
2–12 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Walk the patch yourself before the group comes out — this activity exists only where it honestly can: grass you KNOW is untreated (no sprays, no weed killer, ever — if you do not know, the walk finds something else that day), with ripe clocks actually standing. Count them roughly: a patch with three clocks and twelve kids is a sharing lesson, not a blowing walk, so find the good patch first.

Materials

  • ripe dandelion clocks (the fluffy seed heads) growing on untreated grass
  • no other materials — lungs and eyes only

Steps

  1. Tell the group the old game in one plain breath, named as the tradition it is: 'Long ago, people played a game with these: you blow the seeds off and count your blows — that number was your pretend time. They called it a dandelion clock.' Pretend time, said as pretend — the counting is real, the clock is a game people have played for a very long time.
  2. Finding round: walk slowly and spot clocks — a kid who finds one points and calls 'Clock!' and the group gathers. The finder holds the stem low, with an adult hand steadying it if the ground is uneven, because a clock that gets knocked loses its seeds to the wind instead of the game.
  3. State the one rule before the first blow: 'Blow AWAY from faces, and the clock stays on its stem until it is bare.' The finder blows: one puff, seeds lifting off and floating — the group watches where they go before the next puff, because watching the seeds travel is most of the activity.
  4. Count the puffs together, out loud, one number per blow, until the head is bare or only the stubborn seeds are left: 'Seven puffs — seven o'clock!' by the group's pretend clock. The number belongs to the blower and gets said with ceremony; every blower's time is different and that is correct.
  5. Finish down low: get eyes at seed height and look at the bare head and the seeds landed around it, and say the plain true thing: 'Those seeds are trying to grow new dandelions. Some of them will.' Then the walk goes on — there may be one more clock, and everyone knows how to read it now.

Variations

Easier: One shared clock for the whole group, puffs taken in turns around it — the count still builds blow by blow, and the clock is read together.

Harder: Two clocks side by side: two blowers, two counts, and the group compares the pretend times — earlier clock, later clock — with no faster-or-slower judgement attached.

⚠️ Safety

Untreated grass only, no exceptions — a lawn whose spray history is unknown is not used, and the adult, not the patch, decides. Blowing is always away from faces and eyes; a kid who wants to watch from a step back is watching in full. Seeds and stems are not eaten, hands get washed back inside, and clocks near pathsides where dogs are walked are looked at, not picked.
