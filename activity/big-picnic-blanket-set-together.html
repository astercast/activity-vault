Big Picnic Blanket Set-Together

The crew carries one big blanket in together, spreads it flat, and sets a pretend picnic plate by plate, every place laid by a different pair of hands.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–14 kids
Time
15–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Check the picnic set like equipment: plates and cups unbreakable and BIG, pretend food in single chunky pieces with nothing detachable, and, the record insists, no real food anywhere near this activity, which is a feature to say proudly in your own planning: a pretend picnic can include every child in the room, because nobody's allergy, diet, or family rule is ever sitting on this blanket. Choose the picnic ground with the crew's size in mind: the blanket spread flat needs clearance on all four sides for setters to walk round it without stepping ON it. Stepping on the blanket is prevented by room, not by scolding.

Materials

  • one big picnic blanket (the crew's cargo and then its table)
  • big pretend picnic settings, one per place (a big unbreakable plate, a big cup, one chunky pretend food piece, all far too big for a mouth, no real food anywhere in the set)
  • a basket the settings travel in (the pantry)

Steps

  1. Hold the folded blanket up and say the plain job: 'A picnic needs a crew. This blanket has to be carried to our picnic ground, spread flat by all of us together, and set with a place for everybody, and every plate and cup gets placed by a different person's hands, or it is not a crew picnic.' State the two truths of the day out loud: 'The picnic is PRETEND. There is no real food, and nobody has to pretend to eat anything. We are practising the setting, which is the crew's real work.' The one rule: 'The blanket travels held by many hands, and nobody sits on it until it is set.'
  2. The carry: blanket unfolded to carrying length, a holder every few steps along each long edge, lifted on your count and walked at the crew's slowest member's pace to the picnic ground. The halfway truth of every blanket carry arrives on schedule, an end sags, a holder's grip tires, and the crew stops, re-grips TOGETHER on your count, and walks on. Name it as it happens: 'The blanket told us it needed a stop. Crews listen to their blanket.'
  3. The spreading: at the ground, holders walk the blanket open step by step, then the whole crew does the flattening round, kneeling OUTSIDE the edge, palms smoothing outward from the middle, corner-pullers assigned one per corner, pulling gently on your count until the blanket lies FLAT. A wrinkle that survives two smooths is walked round and negotiated by its corner's neighbours: blanket diplomacy, conducted at floor level, by the people it affects.
  4. The setting: places are laid one at a time from the pantry basket. A setter carries one plate in two hands, walks round the OUTSIDE of the blanket, kneels, and sets it on the blanket's edge nearest them; the next setter brings its cup; the next, its pretend food. The crew inspects as it grows: 'Three places set. Every one by different hands.' A place that lands crooked is adjusted by its own setter, at the crew's request, not by a faster expert: the setting belongs to its setter, crookedness and all.
  5. Finish with the picnic that the setting earned: the whole crew sits round the blanket's edge, on the floor, never on the set places, and the picnic is enjoyed in its pretend entirety: pretend pouring admired, pretend sandwiches complimented to their setters by name. Then the clear-up, which is the set done backwards by the same many hands: settings carried back one at a time to the pantry, the blanket lifted, shaken out by its whole crew over the picnic ground (one shake, together, on the count. The session's great final flourish), folded edge to edge, and carried home.

Variations

Easier: A four-place picnic: a small blanket and four settings, the whole arc, carry, spread, set, clear, inside fifteen minutes for a very small crew.

Harder: A guest-place version: the crew sets one extra place, perfectly, for an honoured guest, a visiting teacher, a class toy, a friend from another room, and must agree together what the guest gets first, a planning meeting held at blanket level.

Safety

The blanket is carried and shaken by the whole crew at walking pace with the adult as one of its holders and its caller: a shake happens once, on the count, over clear ground, with every holder's grip confirmed first, never as a surprise and never with anything or anyone on the blanket. Settings are big, unbreakable, and single-piece; anything cracked or chipped leaves the pantry permanently. Setters walk round the blanket's outside on a clear route: a setter who crosses the blanket steps on the crew's work and on other setters' places, so the route round is marked by the room itself, and the picnic is pretend precisely so that no food, and no food rule, can ever make this crew's table unequal.

Inclusion notes

Every job in the picnic exists at more than one height: the blanket's carry includes a section held from any travelling seat, its edge travelling at exactly that holder's height; the setting can be done from a kneeling pad, from a chair at the blanket's edge, or by a setter directing a partner's hands to the exact spot. 'There... down... the cup beside it', while the place remains wholly the director's laying. The caller job, counting the crew's lifts and the great final shake, belongs to whoever holds it and times the whole picnic.
