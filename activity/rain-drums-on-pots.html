Rain Drums on Pots

Upturned pots and fingertips: drizzle is a patter, a downpour is a rumble, the rain outside, played on the kitchen shelf.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–16 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
No prep needed

Setup

Set the drums yourself before the group sits: pots upturned on a low table or the floor, each one rocked by your hand first. A pot that wobbles will tip mid-downpour and end the weather. Mix sizes on purpose: the biggest pot is the deep sound, the smallest is the bright one, and the group will hear the difference before you name it. Best on a genuinely rainy day, when the real sound is at the window for comparison.

Materials

  • several sturdy pots, pans or big bowls, upturned (adult-checked: stable, no sharp rims)
  • no sticks, hands and fingers only

Steps

  1. Start by listening, not playing: hands in laps, ears on the window if it is really raining. Say the plain framing: 'Rain makes a sound landing on things. Pots make a sound too. We are going to make the rain's sound, our size.' One demonstration pat from you, flat palm, once, so the group hears how quiet a pot can be played.
  2. State the one rule: 'Fingers and soft palms only, and only when your pot is called.' Teach the two weathers with your hands in the air first: DRIZZLE is fingertips, light and slow, pat-pat-pat; DOWNPOUR is flat palms, busy and low. The group practises both in the air before any pot is touched.
  3. Weather round, one pot at a time: you call the weather, 'Drizzle over the garden...', one drummer plays it on their pot while everyone else listens, then the call moves on. Hearing your own pot do weather, alone, is the listening this activity exists for.
  4. Storm round, the big moment, once: weather builds over the whole shelf, drizzle on the small pots, growing, until you raise both hands and call 'DOWNPOUR!' and every pot plays at once for a slow count of five, then your hands fall: '...and the rain stops.' Silence after a downpour, in a room of three-year-olds, is a sound worth sitting in.
  5. Finish by matching the window: if real rain is still falling, the group plays ALONG with it for a moment, at its volume, whatever that is. Then pots are turned right-side up and stacked by their drummers, weather over, kitchen restored.

Variations

Easier: One big pot for the whole group, drummers taking turns around it, the adult's hand keeping the weather calls slow and far apart.

Harder: Add the in-between weather: RAIN (steady palms, medium) between drizzle and downpour. Three weathers to tell apart by ear before a hand moves.

Safety

Hands only, never sticks or utensils, a pot played with fingers cannot be drummed into anyone's fingers beside it, and the volume stays at a level set by palms, not hardware. Pots are adult-checked for sharp rims and dead stability before the group sits; a pot that tips is retired mid-session without discussion. The downpour is five counted seconds, not a free-for-all: ears at three are close to the drums, and the storm ending on the call is part of the weather.

Inclusion notes

A pot played in any way that makes its sound counts in full: flat palm, fingertips, the back of a hand, or a pot resting on a lap tray at the drummer's own height. The weather-caller job, holding up the drizzle or downpour picture in your own hands at the right moment, runs the whole storm, and a caller who never drums has still made the rain happen.
