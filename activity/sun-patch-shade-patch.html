Sun Patch, Shade Patch

A sunny patch and a shady patch side by side, and one job: stand in each one and feel the difference on your own hands.

Ages 3–3 · 10–15 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–3
Group
2–14 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Scout the patches yourself first, an hour matters: you need sun and shade BESIDE each other at the time the group goes out, with the shade big enough for the whole group to stand in. Stand in both yourself, if your own hands cannot feel the difference today, the weather has not provided this activity and it waits for a better day, honestly.

Materials

  • a sunny patch of ground with a shady patch right beside it (under a tree, an awning, or a building's shadow)
  • a rug or mat (the shade stop)

Steps

  1. Gather the group in the shade first and state the one job: 'We are finding out what the sun does. Hands out, palms up. Feel.' Everyone holds their hands out in the shade and names what they feel in whatever words they have, cool, nothing, wind, all answers are weather reports.
  2. Walk the group into the sun patch together and stand still: hands out, palms up, same as before. Give it a real minute. Warmth arrives slowly on skin, and hurrying past it teaches nothing. Then the plain sentence, said together: 'The sun is warm.'
  3. Back to the shade, same still minute, same hands out. The sentence this side: 'The shade is cool.' Two patches, two sentences, felt on the same hands a minute apart. That comparison is the whole science at three, and it is real science.
  4. Choice round: each kid picks their patch, sun or shade, and stands in it while the group calls out where everyone is standing. A kid who keeps swapping patches to feel the change again is doing the activity more, not less.
  5. Finish in the shade with the mat down: sit, hands in laps, and one last call of the two sentences by the whole group, pointing at each patch as it is named. The patches will move by tomorrow. Say so; shadows walking is next year's activity.

Variations

Easier: Two patches only and the adult's hands as the example: hold one hand in sun and one in shade yourself first, and let each kid feel your warm hand and your cool hand.

Harder: Add a middle patch, half sun, half shade across the body, and let kids stand astride the line and report which SIDE of them is warm.

⚠️ Safety

Sun time is short and this activity is why: a minute or two of standing in a sun patch, in hats where the program uses them, and the shade is always the home base. Nobody is left standing in full sun while the group talks. Hot ground is checked with an adult hand first; a patch too hot to stand on in bare legs is a patch for another day.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Feeling the patches works from any height: a seated kid's hands reach the same sun, and the rug in the shade is placed so wheels and walkers are IN the shade patch, not beside it. A kid who cannot say 'warm' can show it. Hand pressed to the adult's cheek from each patch is a weather report the whole group understands.
