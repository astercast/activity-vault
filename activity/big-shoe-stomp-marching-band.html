Big Shoe Stomp Marching Band

Shoes are the drums and the spot is the stage: the band marches on the spot, shakers in hand, and stops dead when the conductor's hand goes up.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–18 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Audit the shakers one by one, shake each hard over a table, listening for any change that says its seal is thinking of leaving, and test the stage with your own feet: the floor must take a stomp without slipping, echoing painfully, or apologising to the room below; if the floor complains, the band takes its stage to the rug, the mat, or outside, and the record says which and why. Practise conducting yourself until your hand is readable at ten feet: UP means stopping, and at three a stopping signal is only real if it is rehearsed, the band learns the hand in the first minute by playing at stopping, before any marching is asked of it.

Materials

  • simple shakers, one per musician (adult-audited: sealed tight, a kind sound at full shake, nothing that can open, spill, or be interesting to a mouth)
  • the conductor's raised hand (no baton needed. The hand IS the baton, and every musician can see it)
  • a clear standing floor, marked if the room needs it (the stage, one spot per musician, elbow room honest on both sides)

Steps

  1. Bring the band to its spots, shakers in hands, and say the plain framing: 'A marching band usually marches somewhere. Ours marches HERE, feet marching on the spot, shakers keeping time, everybody playing together. And a band has one law above all others: when the conductor's hand goes UP, the music STOPS, feet, shakers, voices, all of it, like a statue with the sound turned off.' State the laws whole: 'Stomps land flat and under your own spot, feet march, they do not travel. Shakers shake at waist height, never swung. The stop belongs to everybody the instant the hand rises.'
  2. First stop together, the record's foundation: free stomp-and-shake, everybody, gloriously ragged, five full seconds of it, then raise your hand and hold it until the last shaker dies: name the stop at full size: 'Band. You STOPPED. All of you, together, on one hand. Grown-up bands practise that for years.' Practise twice more, shorter runs, cleaner stops, before a single march step is added: the stop is the band's first instrument, and it is tuned before the music starts.
  3. Marching round: with the stop proven, the march begins, feet lifting and landing on the spot in the adult's called time ('LEFT, right, LEFT, right', chanted as the beat itself), shakers shaking on the landing, the band staying gloriously in place while sounding exactly like travel. The conductor varies the weather of it in the open: 'Softer. The band is marching past a sleeping house... LOUDER. The band is marching into the square!' Each change is a whole-band act of listening, and each stop, when the hand rises mid-march, is received like the trick it is: silence, feet mid-air and all, held for one breath before the melt into grins.
  4. Solo spots, the round every musician waits for: the band stills its feet but keeps a whisper of shaker going, and one musician at a time takes a four-count solo on the spot, stomp pattern, shaker figure, any rhythm that is truly theirs, while the band holds the floor under them and the conductor counts them in and out like a headline act: 'On the drums of her shoes. MAYA.' Four counts is a headline at three, and the record bills it as one, in the programme it says out loud.
  5. Finish with the band's best march yet, full stomp, full shaker, one loudness change, one hush, and a final stop held a breath longer than any before it, feet frozen, shakers silent, the conductor's hand high: then hands down, shakers rested into their basket one musician at a time, counted aloud to their true number, and the tell-back said over the quiet stage: 'The band marched nowhere and arrived everywhere. It stopped as one band, nine times, on one raised hand. That is what bands spend years learning, and this one learned it before snack.'

Variations

Easier: A feet-only version: no shakers, the shoes alone, stops and march only, the whole curriculum inside ten minutes for the newest bands.

Harder: A parade-finish version (a willing corridor, a patient doorway): the last march TRAVELS, slowly, in a line, conductor walking backwards at its head, out of the room and back, the band's one journey, taken at walking pace with stops at every door.

Safety

Stomps land flat, on the spot, under the stomper: marching feet never travel during the rounds, shoes stay on feet for the whole session (a loose shoe is re-fastened by the adult before the band resumes), and spots are spaced an honest elbow's width apart so no shaker's arc meets a neighbour. Shakers are sealed, audited before every session, shaken at waist height, and rested the moment one sounds different. A changed shaker is retired mid-session without ceremony, because its contents on a floor full of marching feet is the session's one real hazard. The stop is also a safety instrument: any stumble, collision, or rising wildness is answered with the raised hand, and the band's practised stillness is the adult's fastest tool in the room. Which is why it is tuned first, every time, before the music.

Source: Wikipedia: Marching band

Inclusion notes

The band is built of whoever is there: a musician's spot can be a chair with feet marching against a footrest or the floor from sitting, a shaker can be worked in a fist, on a tray in strokes, or by directing a partner's shake, 'on MY count: shake, shake, STOP', and every version counts as playing, because the band's music is measured in togetherness, not height. The conductor's job rotates by list and works from any spot: the raised hand can be a raised card, a lifted shaker, or a called 'STOP' for a conductor whose hands are busy. The band stops for the signal, whatever form carries it. Solo spots belong to every musician by the same list, at whatever instrument their hands have chosen.
