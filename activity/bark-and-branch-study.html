Bark and Branch Study

Identify winter trees with no leaves — bark patterns and buds are the clues.

Ages
7–12
Group
2–30 kids
Time
25–45 min
Where
Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Pick a park or schoolyard with a few different trees. Print one ID key per pair.

Materials

  • a winter tree ID key (printed or one shared book)
  • paper and crayons for bark rubbings

Steps

  1. Do bark rubbings: press paper against the trunk, rub sideways with a crayon.
  2. Study the buds at branch tips with the ID key — shape, size, arrangement.
  3. Match each tree to the key and label your rubbings with the tree's name.
  4. Back inside: line up the rubbings and see if the class agrees on each ID.

Variations

Easier: Focus on just two trees; teacher does the ID, kids do the rubbings.

Harder: Keep a winter tree log through the season and watch the buds swell in spring.

⚠️ Safety

Don't pull bark off trees or break branches — rubbings only.
