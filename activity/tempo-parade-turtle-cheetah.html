Tempo Parade: Turtle Slow, Cheetah Fast

A drum sets the speed and little bodies learn it: creep like a turtle, scurry like a cheetah, and stop when the beat stops.

Before you begin

Ages
3–5
Group
6–20 kids
Time
15–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
No prep needed

Setup

Walk the loop path once with the whole group at an ordinary pace so every child knows the route before speed enters the game. Teach the two animal speeds standing still: turtle steps (tiny, heavy, slow, arms swinging low) and cheetah steps (light, quick, on the toes, quiet feet). Set the law of the drum before the first beat: feet follow the drum, and when the drum stops, bodies freeze. Practise the freeze twice with big praise; for this age the stopping is the skill, and the speed is the fun that teaches it.

Materials

  • one hand drum, bucket or pot lid the leader can play at a steady speed
  • a clear loop path around the room or yard, wide enough for slow giants and fast cheetahs to share
  • two picture cards if you have them (a turtle and a cheetah) or two hand signs the group invents on the spot

Steps

  1. Drum slow. The parade creeps the loop as turtles, and the leader names the speed out loud while it happens: this slow beat has a name musicians use, and the name is tempo. Slow tempo, turtle steps.
  2. Drum a little faster, walking pace. The parade walks as people, ordinary speed, and the leader changes the card or the hand sign so the room can see the speed change before they feel it in their feet.
  3. Drum fast. Cheetahs. Keep the fast stretch short (twenty seconds is plenty at this age) and end it with a drum stop and a freeze, every time, so fast never means out of control.
  4. Now the drum changes without warning: slow, fast, medium, stop. The leader watches feet, not faces: the win is a whole parade changing speed together within a few beats.
  5. Hand the drum to a child leader for one loop. The parade follows their tempo, including the stops. The child with the drum learns the other half of the lesson: a tempo is something you give a group, and a group is trusting you to give it clearly.
  6. Finish slow. One last turtle loop, slower and slower, until the drum fades to single taps and the parade melts down to sitting. Name the day in one line: we moved at the drum's tempo, slow like turtles and fast like cheetahs, and we stopped together.

Variations

Easier: One speed change only (turtle and freeze), a shorter loop, and the leader drums beside the group rather than ahead of it.

Harder: Add a third animal with its own in-between speed (a trotting horse works), and let children call the changes with the picture cards while the leader drums what they show.

Safety

Feet stay on the floor at every speed: cheetahs run in place or along the path with walking feet if the room is excited or the floor is slick. Fast is a tempo, not a race.

Source: Wikipedia, en.wikipedia.org

Why it works

Tempo is the first element of music young children can control with their whole bodies; matching movement speed to a heard beat builds the steady-beat foundation every later music skill stands on.

Inclusion notes

A child who moves by wheelchair or walker sets their own turtle and cheetah speeds on the same drum; the parade matches the drum, never each other. A child who finds drumming sounds hard can hold the picture cards and be the signal-keeper instead.
