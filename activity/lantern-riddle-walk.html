Lantern Riddle Walk

Write a riddle, hang it on a lantern, walk the row and solve everyone else's, the Lantern Festival way.

Before you begin

Ages
6–12
Group
4–30 kids
Time
25–40 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Make or fold the lanterns first (paper-lantern-festival is the craft if you need one), and string a line across the room at kid height. Write one sample riddle yourself. Answer hidden under a flap.

Materials

  • paper lanterns (simple folded-paper ones work)
  • markers
  • string and tape for hanging
  • slips of paper for answers

Steps

  1. Teach the tradition plainly: at the Lantern Festival, which closes Lunar New Year celebrations, riddles are written on lanterns for passers-by to solve. That is the game, unchanged.
  2. Each kid writes a riddle on their lantern, about an object in the room, an animal, a classmate's desk item. Answer goes under a taped flap or on the back.
  3. Hang every lantern on the line. Lights low if you can; even ordinary light works.
  4. Walk the row in pairs with an answer slip. Solve quietly, check the flap, move on. No shouting answers, the next pair deserves the puzzle.
  5. Gather and vote: hardest riddle, funniest riddle, fairest riddle. The writers reveal themselves last.

Variations

Easier: Picture riddles for younger kids. Draw the clues, write the answer word together.

Harder: Require a rhyming riddle, or a two-step riddle whose first answer feeds the second.

Safety

String lines stay at kid height and out of walkways; lanterns are paper only, never near a heat source or real flame.

Source: Wikipedia, en.wikipedia.org

Inclusion notes

Riddles on lanterns are a real Lantern Festival custom. Name it, place it (the 15th day of Lunar New Year), and play it as written rather than dressing it up. Guessing is in pairs so nobody is stuck alone.
