Pass-It-Down Delivery Line

A line of hands moves soft picnic things, one at a time, into the basket, every hand touches every delivery.

Ages 3–5 · 10–20 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–5
Group
4–20 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Stand the basket at one end of the room and the pile of soft things at the other. The line stands (or sits) between them, close enough that a small arm can hand to the next small arm without a stretch. Move the kids, not the standard: if a handoff needs a lunge, the gap is too wide.

Materials

  • a basket or box
  • soft things to deliver: plush fruit, beanbags, rolled socks, a soft toy loaf (8-12 items)

Steps

  1. Show the one rule with the first item, walking it down the line yourself hand to hand: 'Hands only, no throwing. The next hands have to be ready.' Ready hands are held out, palms up.
  2. Start the deliveries one at a time. The caller (you, at first) names the item as it sets off, 'The apple is travelling!', and the line watches it travel.
  3. When the basket end receives an item, it goes in the basket with a small ceremony: held up, named, dropped in. The line applauds its own delivery.
  4. Speed comes by itself and needs no help. When the line is smooth, send two items at once, a few hands apart, so every kid is both receiving and passing.
  5. Finish by tipping the basket out together and counting the deliveries back onto the pile, every item made the trip, and every kid can be asked which one their hands carried best.

Variations

Easier: A shorter line (four kids), a bigger basket opening, and the adult as the permanent middle hand.

Harder: Add the quiet round: the whole line delivers in silence, listening to the small sounds soft things make, then a backwards round, basket to pile.

⚠️ Safety

Soft things only, if it would hurt dropped on a bare foot, it does not travel. Nothing goes over heads; a dropped item is picked up by the nearest hands, never dived for.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The line works seated, standing, or mixed. Chairs in the line are part of the line, not a special arrangement. A kid who passes with one hand, with two, or by sliding the item along a tray table is passing, full stop; the rule is hands ready, and ready looks different on different bodies.
