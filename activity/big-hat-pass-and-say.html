Big Hat Pass and Say

One big soft hat travels hand to hand while the grown-up sings; when the song stops, whoever holds it puts it on and says their name and one favourite thing.

Ages
3–3
Group
5–18 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Know your hat before the circle does: it must be soft all through (no stiff brim edge at face height), clean enough to pass head to head — it gets a wash before its first circle and between groups as a matter of course — and big enough that wearing it is never a squeeze. Rehearse your stopping song too: any song the group knows, sung by you, that you can end mid-line without apology, because the stop is the mechanism and it must be allowed to arrive anywhere.

Materials

  • one big soft hat (floppy, clean, big enough to sit loosely on any head in the room — the sillier the better, and it belongs to the group, not to a costume box's depths)
  • no other materials — the grown-up's singing voice is the music

Steps

  1. Hold the hat up and say the plain bargain: 'This is the talking hat. While I sing, the hat travels — hand to hand, round the circle. When my song STOPS, the hands holding the hat keep it, and that person puts it on and tells us two things: their name, and one favourite thing. Then the singing starts again.' State the one rule: 'The hat travels hand to hand. It is never thrown, and it only goes on a head when the song stops.'
  2. First stop together, rigged kindly: sing, pass the hat hand to hand yourself with the first holders, and stop the song with the hat in YOUR hands. On it goes, and you model the whole introduction at full size: 'My name is Teacher Sam, and my favourite thing is puddles.' Two sentences, said proudly, by a person wearing a ridiculous hat — the room now knows exactly what courage is required, and exactly how small it is.
  3. Passing rounds: the song restarts, the hat travels — sometimes three hands, sometimes right round the circle — and every stop produces one wearer and one introduction, received by the circle with the group's own growing ceremony (a clap, a cheer, the name said back: all traditional by the third stop). A wearer may pass on the favourite and give only their name; the hat accepts, the circle accepts, and the record notes that a name alone, said aloud in a big hat, is a complete introduction at three.
  4. Hat-respect moment, arriving on schedule: the hat will be grabbed for, worn mid-song, or worn by three heads at once in a pile-up of enthusiasm, usually by week two of knowing it. The rule is restated as hat law, kindly: 'The hat is one person's at a time, and its person is chosen by the song stopping. The hat is patient. We are learning to be patient like the hat.' It lands, because by then the hat has a personality and the room is fond of it.
  5. Finish when the hat has been worn by everyone who wants a turn — want being the operative word, checked honestly round the circle — with a final all-group photograph taken in memory: every introduced name said back in order round the circle by the group, as the hat makes one last slow lap held high. Then the hat goes to its hook, where it lives between circles: furniture now, not a prop, and rooms treat it accordingly.

Variations

Easier: A small circle of five and a short song: the hat completes the whole circle inside ten minutes and every intro gets its full ceremony.

Harder: Favourite-thing themes: today's hat asks for a favourite FOOD, tomorrow's a favourite ANIMAL — the introduction grows a category, and returning wearers must reach for a new answer.

⚠️ Safety

The hat is soft, group-owned, and washed before its first circle and between groups — a hat that travels head to head is laundry with a job, and it is treated that way. It is passed hand to hand, never thrown: a thrown hat at three is followed by thrown everything, and the hat's dignity is the circle's. Wearing is always by the wearer's own hands at the song's stop; nobody places the hat on another person's head, and a holder who does not want to wear it may hold it on their lap and introduce themselves to the hat instead, which counts completely.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The hat's circle includes every seat as it stands: it travels lap to lap and tray to tray as faithfully as hand to hand, and its stopping-place is wherever it stops, honoured identically. An introduction arrives by voice, by sign, by a picture card held up for the favourite thing, or by the adult voicing what the wearer indicates — the hat's two questions answered in any language the circle can receive. The song-keeper job, choosing which song the hat travels to, belongs to whoever holds it and programs the whole session.

    Ages 3–3 · 10–15 min

  2. Big Pass the Smile Circle

    Ages 3–3 · 10–15 min

  3. Hello Ball Greetings

    Ages 3–5 · 10–15 min
