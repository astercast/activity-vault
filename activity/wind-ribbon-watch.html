Wind Ribbon Watch

Ribbons tied along a fence by a grown-up, and one job: watch them, point when a gust comes, and say which way the wind is pushing.

Ages
3–6
Group
2–16 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Tie the ribbons up yourself before the group comes out: along one fence or rail, spaced out, each tied short enough that it cannot wrap around anything and long enough to stream properly — waist-height on an adult is about right for a seated group to watch. Tug every knot once; a ribbon that comes away in your hand gets retied now.

Materials

  • six to ten long fabric ribbons or strips (adult-tied along a fence, rail, or low branch)
  • a rug or blanket to sit on

Steps

  1. Sit the group on the rug facing the ribbons and state the one job: 'The ribbons tell us what the wind is doing. When a gust comes, point at the ribbons and call it.' Model the first call yourself, big and pleased: 'Gust!' — pointing is the participation, and it starts with you.
  2. Quiet watching round: everyone watches and nobody calls for the first minute. Ribbons hanging still, then stirring, then streaming — noticing the in-between states is the observation skill here, and it needs a quiet minute to show up.
  3. Calling round: gusts get pointed at and called by whoever spots the movement first, and the group answers the call by looking where the finger points. There is no score; a call everyone checks is a call that worked.
  4. Direction round: when a good gust streams all the ribbons one way, say the plain fact together: 'The wind is pushing them THAT way' — and everyone points the way the ribbons lean. Same wind, same direction, said as one group.
  5. Finish with the still test: the group watches for the moment every ribbon hangs completely still, and the kid who calls 'still!' first is believed only when the whole group agrees it is true — then one last gust usually ruins it, which is the correct ending and everyone knows it.

Variations

Easier: Three ribbons only, tied close together, and the adult calls the first three gusts — a smaller picture to watch.

Harder: Two ribbon spots in different places (fence and branch): the group watches one, then the other, and says which spot has more wind today.

⚠️ Safety

Ribbons are tied by an adult, out of reach of necks and climbing — nobody pulls ribbons down, ties them on themselves, or winds them around hands or wrists. The group stays seated on the rug during watching rounds; gusts are called with voices and fingers, not chased.
