Big Funnel Sand Pour

A big funnel in a bottle, a cup of dry sand, and a stream that pours, narrows and fills — watch the level climb from the bottom up.

Ages
3–3
Group
2–8 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Build the station on the tray and test it with your own pour first: the funnel must sit steady in the bottle's mouth without being held — a funnel that needs a holding hand leaves the pourer one-handed, so wedge or choose until it stands alone. Sand is dry and clean, the cup is small enough that a pour finishes, and the whole station lives on the tray: sand that reaches the tray has not escaped, it has arrived.

Materials

  • one big wide-mouth funnel per pourer (adult-checked, smooth rim)
  • a clear bottle or jar with the funnel seated in it
  • dry sand in a big pouring cup
  • a deep tray under everything (the sand's whole world)

Steps

  1. Show the empty bottle first and say the plain question the whole activity answers: 'The sand is in the cup. The bottle's mouth is little. How does the sand get in? Slowly — through the funnel. Watch where it lands.' Hold the funnel up alone once: wide top, narrow bottom, one hole — the shape is the explanation.
  2. State the one rule: 'Pour slow, over the funnel, and stop when I say the cup is empty — then we LOOK before anyone pours again.' First pour together: sand goes in a thin stream, and the group watches the pile inside the bottle start at the bottom and build — 'The bottle fills from the bottom up. The top fills last.'
  3. Pouring round: each pourer tips their own cup, at their own pace, keeping the stream inside the funnel's wide mouth. A stream that misses teaches aim better than any correction — the tray catches the evidence and the next pour adjusts itself. Between pours, the group checks the level: finger on the glass at the sand line, number of cups counted out loud.
  4. Full-and-finish moment: when the sand reaches the funnel's neck and slows to a stop, leave it there a moment and look — the bottle full, the funnel holding the last of the stream back. Say the earned sentence: 'The funnel let it in a little at a time. That is the funnel's whole job.'
  5. Reset is the second half of the science and the group does it: the adult lifts the bottle, the pourers hold the big cup, sand comes back out — faster upside down than it went in, which somebody will notice, and the noticing is the lesson landing twice. Tray sand is scooped back into the cup by hand, every grain accounted for by small fingers.

Variations

Easier: A bigger funnel and a measuring jug instead of a bottle, three cups only, with the adult's hand steadying nothing but staying under the funnel as a safety net that never touches.

Harder: Two funnels, two bottles, two sands of different colours poured side by side: same cups, and the group judges which bottle's level climbed faster — and argues about why, which is the point.

⚠️ Safety

Sand stays on the tray and in the station — never thrown, never blown, and hands that have been in sand stay away from faces until they are washed; sand in an eye is the one real accident this activity can have, and the tray rule is what prevents it. Pouring is done seated or standing still at the tray, never carried around the room. The bottle is plastic or the adult's hands move it, always: glass and sand and small pourers meet only through an adult.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The pour is the pourer's at any height: the whole tray station works on a lap tray or a low table edge, the cup can be tipped two-handed or with its handle braced on the funnel's rim, and an adult pouring at a kid's direction — 'pour... keep pouring... stop!' — is a full experiment where the director called every variable. The level-checker job, finger on the glass calling the count, is the measurement itself.
