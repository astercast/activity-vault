Eight Lights Line Up

Eight LED lights in a row, put on one at a time and counted as they go. The Hanukkah lights, at a three-year-old's pace.

Ages 3–6 · 10–20 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–6
Group
2–16 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Set the row first: a long tray or a taped line on a low table, with eight marked spots along it, tape crosses work, and count the spots out loud yourself as you mark them. Test every LED light before the group sits down; a light that will not switch on gets swapped now, not discovered by a kid mid-count.

Materials

  • nine LED tealights or LED candles (eight plus one helper light)
  • a long tray, board, or taped line on the table (the row)

Steps

  1. Set the scene in two plain sentences: 'On Hanukkah, families light candles on a holder called a menorah. One more light each night, for eight nights. We are putting our eight lights in a row, all in one go, and counting them as they light.' Put the helper light at the start of the row and switch it on: 'This one is the helper. It is not counted in the eight.'
  2. State the one rule: 'One light at a time. We count it, then the next one.' The first kid places the first light on the first spot, switches it on, and the whole group counts 'one' together. The counting is done by everyone, every time.
  3. Work down the row the same way, a different kid placing each light where they can: place, switch, count up from one together. Recounting from one every time is not inefficiency at three. It is the counting practice this activity is built around.
  4. When all eight are lit, sit back and count the whole row once more, pointing at each light in turn, then name the helper again so the eight stays honest: eight counted lights, one helper, nine lights shining.
  5. Lights-out round, in reverse: the last light placed is the first switched off, counted down, eight, seven, six, by the group, each light carried back to the basket by its placer. The count works both ways and the row ends empty.

Variations

Easier: Four lights instead of eight, with the same one-at-a-time rule and a full recount after each. A shorter row is still a real row.

Harder: No marked spots: the placer has to judge the spacing so eight lights fit the tray, and the group helps with 'a bit more room' calls.

⚠️ Safety

LED lights only, never real candles or matches, the record says why in plain words if a kid asks: real flames are a grown-up job away from this table. LED tealights with screw-closed battery covers only; a light with a loose cover is retired on the spot, because button batteries are a serious hazard for this age.
