Go Fish

'Do you have any sevens?' The first card game every child learns, and still the best way to teach asking.

Before you begin

Ages
4–10
Group
2–6 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
No prep needed

Setup

One ordinary deck, jokers out. Deal five cards to each player (seven each when only two play) and scatter the rest face down in a loose pile in the middle: the ocean. Young players may fan their cards face up on the table with a partner's help; the game forgives it completely.

Materials

  • a standard deck of cards

Steps

  1. On your turn, choose one player and ask for a rank you already hold: 'Do you have any threes?' You may only ask for what is in your own hand.
  2. Holding that rank? They hand over every card of it, and you ask again. A good ask earns another turn.
  3. Holding none? They say 'Go fish!', and you draw one card from the ocean. Draw the rank you asked for and you show it and take another turn; otherwise your turn ends.
  4. Four cards of one rank make a book. Lay books face up in front of you the moment they are complete.
  5. When the ocean is empty and the hands run out, count the books. Most books wins, and the player with the fewest deals the rematch.

Variations

Easier: Play in pairs with cards face up on the table, asking together, or play to pairs instead of books of four.

Harder: The memory rule for older players: nothing may be written down, and a player caught asking for a rank they handed away earlier loses their next turn.

Safety

Cards are paper; tempers are the only hazard in the game. Keep the asking cheerful and the answers honest, and make rematches instant: the second game of Go Fish is where the learning lives.
