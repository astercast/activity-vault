Sponge Squeeze Station

Two tubs, one sponge, and a fill line to reach — moving water one squeeze at a time is the whole job, and it's enough.

Ages
3–5
Group
1–6 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Put a strip of tape on the inside of the empty tub to mark a fill line. Fill the other tub halfway with water and set both on a tray or a towel. This is a small-group station — two or three kids at a time while the rest of the room does something else.

Materials

  • two shallow tubs or bowls
  • big soft sponges (one per kid)
  • water
  • a towel (for the spills that are part of it)
  • tape (for a fill line)

Steps

  1. Show the squeeze once, slowly: sponge in the water, lift it dripping, hold it over the empty tub, squeeze until it stops. The squeeze is the skill; everything else is watching water move.
  2. Kids take turns moving water sponge by sponge. Nobody hurries — a sponge squeezed over the wrong tub just gets squeezed again.
  3. Watch the empty tub climb toward the tape line. Ask the only question that matters: 'Is it there yet? How can you tell?'
  4. When the line is reached, reverse it: all the water goes home, squeeze by squeeze, back where it started.
  5. Wring the sponges one last time, carry the water out together (an adult carries the tub), and the towel squad pats the tray dry.

Variations

Easier: Smaller tubs and a lower fill line; a sponge cut in half fits small hands better.

Harder: Two fill lines and a prediction first — 'How many squeezes to the first line?' — then count the squeezes for real.

⚠️ Safety

Water stays in the tubs and spills get toweled straight away — wet floors are slippery. Hands get washed when the station closes.
