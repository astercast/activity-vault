Sticky Window Big Shapes

Clear sticky film on a low window, sticky side out, and big shapes to press on with a whole palm — a picture you can move all day.

Ages
3–3
Group
2–14 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Mount the film yourself and take your time over it: sticky side out, edges taped down ALL the way round so no small fingers can peel the film itself up, at a height where a standing kid's palm reaches the middle and a seated kid's palm reaches the bottom third. Test it with the biggest, softest piece you have — tissue that will not hold gets swapped for fabric before the group arrives, because a piece that falls teaches the window is broken.

Materials

  • clear contact paper / sticky-back film (adult-taped to a low window or smooth board, sticky side OUT)
  • big shapes to stick: tissue-paper pieces, fabric scraps, big leaves, felt shapes (all adult-cut, palm-size or bigger)
  • a tray or basket for the pieces

Steps

  1. Show the window working before anyone is handed a piece: press one big shape on yourself with a flat palm, hold, let go — it stays. Then peel it off and press it somewhere else. Say the plain wonder of it out loud: 'It sticks, and it UN-sticks. You can move your picture all day. Nothing here can be put in the wrong place.'
  2. State the one rule: 'Press with your whole hand, flat.' First pieces go up one kid at a time so everyone sees a palm-press work — a fingertip poke crumples tissue, a palm lays it flat, and the difference is visible across the room.
  3. Making round: pieces travel from basket to window at each kid's own pace, pressed, looked at, peeled, moved. Overlaps are fine and get named kindly — 'Your leaf is sleeping on the red one' — because a shared window is a group picture whether anyone planned it that way or not.
  4. Step-back round, halfway: everyone takes two steps back — or wheels back — and just looks at the window together, light coming through the tissue pieces. Naming what you can see in it (a sun, a fish, nothing at all yet) is looking practice, and the light does half the showing.
  5. Finish open-ended on purpose: the window STAYS UP. Pieces can be moved at any free moment for the rest of the day, and the last job — whenever the film finally comes down — is the group peeling their own pieces off into the basket, because an un-sticking round is as satisfying as the sticking one and the pieces keep for next time.

Variations

Easier: Three big fabric pieces only, on the lowest third of the film, with the adult holding the basket at elbow height — choosing and pressing is the whole craft.

Harder: A loose theme offered, never set: 'The window is hungry for green things today' — and the basket quietly holds mostly leaves and green pieces for kids who want a brief.

⚠️ Safety

The film's edges are taped by an adult and checked each session: peeled-up film corners are the one real hazard here (sticky film over a face is no joke) and a lifting edge retires the window until it is re-taped. Pieces are palm-size or bigger — no glitter, no sequins, nothing small and shiny at this table. Pressing is done with hands only; nobody leans their weight or their forehead on the glass behind the film.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The bottom third of the window is the seated artists' section by design, not by leftovers: it catches the same light, and pieces pressed from a chair or a lap tray are in the picture in full. A kid can also work as the piece-keeper, holding the basket and handing each artist their chosen shape — handing over the right piece, by name, is gallery work.
