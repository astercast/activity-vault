Cup of Snow Melting Watch

One clear cup of real snow, brought inside and watched: it goes down, the water comes up, and nobody touches it. Waiting is the activity.

Ages
3–6
Group
2–16 kids
Time
15–25 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Collect the snow yourself from a clean patch away from paths, roads and anywhere salt or grit has been spread — if you would not trust it near a kid's mittens, do not scoop it. Fill the clear cup heaped, set it where the whole group can see it, and draw the marker line at the snow's top edge where everyone watches you draw it.

Materials

  • a clear cup or jar
  • clean fresh snow (collected by an adult)
  • a marker (for the level line)
  • a spoon (adult's, for the collecting)

Steps

  1. Start with hands behind backs and eyes only: 'What do we think will happen to the snow inside?' Take every answer — smaller, water, gone — and say the plan plainly: 'We are going to watch it happen. Nobody touches the cup. The watching is the job.'
  2. While it melts, go and look at where the snow came from: the same snow, still outside, still white. Coming back to a changed cup after seeing the unchanged patch is what makes the change land at three.
  3. First check after a few minutes: the level has dropped below the marker line. Draw the new line together — the kid with the steadiest hand holds the cup still with two hands while you draw, or you hold and they point at the exact height.
  4. Patience round: name what the water at the bottom used to be. 'That water was the top of the cup.' Say it a few ways; the idea that snow and water are the same thing in two states is the whole science here, and it arrives by looking, not by worksheet.
  5. When the cup is all water, hold it beside a second cup you fill with water to the same mark: same thing, two roads in. The snow cup goes outside to be tipped onto the garden — the water goes home — and the group picks where.

Variations

Easier: A smaller cup (it melts faster) and just two checks: the first line, and the all-water moment.

Harder: Two cups side by side — one by the window in the sun, one in the shade — and the group checks both: same snow, different speeds, and the race needs no winner announced, the lines say it.

⚠️ Safety

Snow is collected by an adult from a clean patch away from roads and salt, and melt water is never drunk — it is looked at, marked, and poured out. Cold hands are part of holding the cup; if a kid says their hands are too cold, the cup goes down and that answer is final.

🤝 Inclusion notes

A kid can do this whole activity by feel as well as sight: the cup is cold at the start and less cold as it melts, and holding it with two hands at each check tells the same story the marker lines tell. Reports can be a word, a point, or a hand raised or lowered to show the level — the melting does not care how it is reported.
