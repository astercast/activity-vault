Big Heart Hand Delivery

One big paper heart, carried in two hands across the room and handed to one named friend — a valentine delivered by hand, at walking pace.

Ages
3–3
Group
4–16 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Cut the hearts yourself beforehand — palm-and-a-half size, plain card, no glitter, no small stickers — and keep your own delivery list where only you can see it: who hands a heart to whom, arranged so every kid gives exactly one and receives exactly one. That list is the whole fairness system of this activity, and it lives with the adult so no three-year-old has to hold it. Hearts wait in the basket; trays wait at seats.

Materials

  • big card or foam hearts (adult-cut, each bigger than two kid hands, one per kid plus spares)
  • a basket (the hearts start here)
  • a tray or mat at each seat (the delivery spots)

Steps

  1. Name the real thing in one plain sentence: 'On Valentine's Day, people give cards to people they are glad to know. Ours are big, and we deliver them by hand.' State the one rule: 'Two hands on your heart, walking feet, and you hand it over — never throw it, never drop it in a lap and run.'
  2. First delivery together, yours: take a heart in two hands, walk it slowly to its person, hold it out at their height, and wait until their two hands have it before yours let go. Say the whole sentence out loud — 'This heart is for you' — because the words are the delivery as much as the card is.
  3. Deliveries round: one carrier at a time, name called by you from the list, heart carried in two hands, handed over hand to hand, thank-you said back. One at a time is not slow — it is what makes each delivery an event the whole room watches, and watching a friend receive theirs is half of receiving your own.
  4. Received hearts go flat on the receiver's tray, where they stay visible for the rest of the session: a room slowly filling with one heart at every seat is the picture this activity is making, and kids will keep checking that theirs is still there. Let them.
  5. Finish with the full-room look: everyone seated behind their heart, and you name what the list already knows — every person in this room gave one heart and every person got one. Then hearts go home in bags or stay on trays for pickup time, carrier's choice, because a delivered valentine belongs to its receiver now.

Variations

Easier: Short route only: the receiver sits two seats away, and the adult walks the first delivery beside the carrier, hands ready underneath the heart without touching it.

Harder: A return round: each receiver carries their heart BACK to its giver with a thank-you said in their own words — delivering and returning are two different walks and both count.

⚠️ Safety

Hearts are big, plain card — nothing small, shiny or stuck-on goes on them, because a valentine at three gets hugged, waved and occasionally tasted. Deliveries are walked, one carrier moving at a time: a room of three-year-olds all carrying at once is a collision arranged in advance. Names are said kindly and hearts are handed over, never snatched back — a heart given stays given.

🤝 Inclusion notes

A delivery counts however it travels: a carrier can wheel their heart on a lap tray, carry it seated-to-seated along a row, or hand it to the adult as a co-carrier who walks it the last step with them — the hand-over, hand-to-hand, is the delivery, and it happens in full every way. A kid who would rather not carry can be the name-caller with the adult, reading the room one delivery at a time — the list-keeper runs the valentines.
