Indigenous Authors Read-Aloud

Picture books written by Indigenous authors, read aloud properly, then one page of response from every listener.

Before you begin

Ages
5–10
Group
4–30 kids
Time
25–35 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Borrow ahead, and check the cover before you commit: the author should be Indigenous and their nation named. Solid picks: 'Fry Bread' by Kevin Noble Maillard (Seminole Nation), 'We Are Water Protectors' by Carole Lindstrom (Turtle Mountain Ojibwe), illustrated by Michaela Goade (Tlingit), and Goade's 'Berry Song'.

Materials

  • one or two picture books by Indigenous authors (from the library)
  • paper (one sheet per kid)
  • crayons or markers

Steps

  1. Show the cover and read the author's name and nation aloud before page one. Who tells the story is part of the lesson, and it takes ten seconds.
  2. Read the book slowly, showing every picture. These books were built to be looked at. Rushing the art is skipping half the text.
  3. Read one favorite page a second time. Let the group vote on which page. Hearing a loved passage twice is a real reading habit, not a delay.
  4. Response page: each kid draws the moment that stayed with them and writes one sentence under it. What happened, or a question they still have.
  5. Share in pairs, then put the books where kids can reach them all week. A read-aloud that ends in re-reading did its job.

Variations

Easier: One short book, drawing only. The sentence can be dictated to an adult or partner.

Harder: Add one sourced fact about the author's nation (where it is, its own name for itself) to the response page, from the book's back pages or the publisher's page.

Source: not listed yet.

Inclusion notes

The rule is authorship: stories about Indigenous people written by Indigenous authors, with their nations named, not legends retold from outside. Present-day settings sit alongside traditional ones in these books, because Indigenous life is not only in the past tense.
