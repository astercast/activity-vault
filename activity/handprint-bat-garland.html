Handprint Bat Garland

Two handprints make bat wings — string them into a classroom garland.

Ages
4–10
Group
2–30 kids
Time
20–35 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Cut black paper into pieces big enough for two handprints. Put out white crayons for tracing.

Materials

  • black construction paper
  • white crayons or chalk
  • scissors
  • string or yarn
  • tape or a hole punch

Steps

  1. Trace both hands on the black paper — fingers spread wide. These are the bat's wings.
  2. Cut out the hands (younger kids: an adult helps with scissors).
  3. Cut a small oval for the body and two triangle ears; glue them between the wings.
  4. Draw a friendly face with the white crayon.
  5. String everyone's bats onto yarn and hang the garland across the room.

Variations

Easier: The teacher pre-cuts handprint pairs; kids just decorate and string them.

Harder: Add a research card to each bat: one real bat fact (they eat insects, they hang upside down).

⚠️ Safety

Scissors stay at the table and cut paper only — adults help the youngest cutters.
