Big Pot Stir-Together

One big empty pot, one long spoon passed hand to hand: everyone adds one pretend thing and stirs it in. Rainy-day soup, made of turns.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–14 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
No prep needed

Setup

Set the pot where the whole group can reach it, middle of a low table or on the floor rug with everyone seated round it, and test it yourself: it must sit dead steady when stirred hard by an enthusiastic cook, because a pot that walks across the table teaches chasing, not stirring. The spoon is long enough that a stirring hand stays clear of the pot's rim. Nothing goes in the pot. That is not a shortage; it is the recipe.

Materials

  • one big sturdy pot or mixing bowl (empty, clean, too big to tip)
  • one long-handled spoon (the stirrer)
  • nothing else. The ingredients are pretend

Steps

  1. Tell the group the situation plainly: 'It is raining, we are inside, and we are making soup. Our soup is pretend soup. Nothing real goes in the pot, and nobody tastes it. What goes in is ideas, one per cook.' State the one rule: 'The spoon travels hand to hand. Only the cook holding the spoon stirs.'
  2. First stir together, yours: name your ingredient out loud. 'I am putting in... a carrot', mime dropping it in, then stir five slow circles while the group counts the circles with you: the count is how the room knows a stir is finished, and five is a stir a three-year-old can complete without the pot becoming a drum.
  3. Cooks' round: the spoon passes, each cook names one thing going in, real foods, silly foods, one memorable dinosaur, drops it in by mime, and stirs their five counted circles. A named ingredient is never corrected: dinosaur soup is a legitimate soup in this kitchen, and the group will defend that position.
  4. Halfway taste-check, performed entirely in mime: you lift the spoon, blow on it, pretend-sip, and deliver the verdict, 'Needs one more thing', and let the group shout suggestions before the next cook chooses. The verdict is always nearly there; a soup that is finished ends the stirring, and nobody wants that.
  5. Finish when every cook has stirred: the last cook gives five final circles, the spoon is laid across the pot like a lid, and the group names the soup by its most famous ingredient. The name gets said with ceremony, because they cooked it. Pot and spoon are washed by the adult where the group can see: pretend kitchens clean up too.

Variations

Easier: Two cooks at a time sharing the spoon. One holds the pot steady with both hands (a real job the pot genuinely needs) while the other stirs, then they swap.

Harder: Counting up: later cooks stir as many circles as their turn number, cook three stirs three, cook six stirs six, with the group counting every circle out loud.

Safety

Nothing real goes in the pot and nothing is tasted, at any point. The rule is said at the start and holds when a helpful cook offers an actual snack from their pocket. The pot is stirred from a seated position only, spoon moving in circles inside it: a spoon waved standing up is a spoon at face height for the cook beside you. Hot is never part of the pretend. The soup is warm in the story and the pot is cold in the hand, and the record says so in case a substitute asks.

Inclusion notes

Every cook cooks: the spoon can be stirred two-handed, rested against the pot's edge for leverage, or held with an adult's hand under the cook's. The five circles are still theirs. A cook who names their ingredient by pointing at a picture, a sign, or the adult's offered choices has named it in full, and a pot-holder whose two hands keep the whole kitchen steady is cooking with both of them.
