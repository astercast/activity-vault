Big Beach Ball, Keep It Floating

The whole circle sits down, hands up: one big soft beach ball is tapped gently into the air, and the circle's one job is to not let it come home to the floor.

Before you begin

Ages
3–3
Group
4–16 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Blow the ball up properly and judge it like equipment: under-blown it flops and dies mid-air (the session's rhythm dies with it), over-blown it stings a palm and startles the circle out of its gentleness. Drop it from head height onto the floor in front of you: it should bounce once, softly, and come to rest thinking about it. Then test the falling, toss it high and time its descent against your own breathing: a beach ball's whole virtue at three is that it gives the room TIME, and a ball that dives is just a ball, and gets swapped before the circle ever sits down. Set the court: seats close, a clear floor inside the circle for the ball's landings, and nothing behind the players that a leaning reach can pull down.

Materials

  • one big beach ball, well blown up (the biggest, softest ball the cupboard owns. It must fall SLOWLY and land like a rumour; the adult has tossed it at their own face, kindly, and been unhurt and unbothered)
  • a circle of seated players with room to lean (the court. Chairs or floor spots set close enough that the ball always has a next pair of hands)

Steps

  1. Seat the circle, ball in your hands in the middle, and say the plain framing: 'This ball is light and slow and it LIKES being up. Our job, all of us together, is to keep it floating: when it comes to you, tap it, gently, UP, with flat hands, and send it on its travels. It will come down sometimes. Down is not losing. Down is where it rests, and then we lift it and it flies again.' State the laws whole: 'Taps are gentle and upward, no hitting down, no grabbing and holding, no throwing at anybody. Players stay seated: the ball travels, we don't. Hands that miss are not wrong hands, the ball chose its road.'
  2. First flight together, yours: tap the ball up from the circle's middle, high and slow, straight into the air above the seats, and narrate its falling honestly while twenty eyes track it: 'Up it goes... it is thinking... it is coming down to... JONAH'S side, flat hands, Jonah, TAP.' The first successful return tap gets the record's full celebration, out of proportion and meant: 'IT STAYED UP. The circle did that. One tap is all it takes and the tap has to come from whoever it visits. That is the whole game, and you are already playing it.' A first flight that lands on the floor inside three taps is equally the curriculum: 'Down. It rested. We lift it, and it flies.' The record's line is said NOW, early, so no landing later can surprise the room.
  3. Floating round: the ball travels the circle in the air's own order, tapped up by whoever it visits, drifting across the middle on the room's draughts, sometimes visiting two players in a row, sometimes resting on the floor for a breath before the nearest hands lift it back into weather. The adult keeps the session's one statistic, said aloud like sports commentary at nap volume: the count of taps before a landing, kept honestly and celebrated at whatever size it grows: 'That flight had SIX taps. Six times somebody's hands were ready.' Counting the flight, never scoring the players, is this record's constitution: the number belongs to the circle, and every hand in it owns a share.
  4. Two-ball stretch, offered only to a circle whose one ball is truly flying: a second beach ball joins the air, 'TWO weathers at once', and the circle's job doubles in the only way it can: more eyes up, more readiness, and landings twice as forgiven, because two balls resting is twice the lifting and the record's line scales without changing: 'Down is where they rest. Up is where this circle sends them.' The stretch is withdrawn the moment it stops being gentle, the adult's call, made kindly and final, and one ball is retired to applause like a player leaving the field.
  5. Finish with the longest flight the circle can give the ball together, taps counted in chorus now, the room's voice rising with the number, and when it lands, it lands: the ball is gathered into the middle, held up once for the room's regard, and deflated another day (today it retires blown up, to the cupboard's top, where floating things live). The tell-back is said over the resting ball: 'It went up forty times and came down forty times and it was a game BOTH ways, because this circle knows how to send a thing back up. The ball is resting now. It earned it. So did your hands.'

Variations

Easier: A four-player version: a small circle, a medium ball, taps counted to ten and celebrated there, the whole record inside ten minutes.

Harder: A standing-garden version (a settled group, a calm day): the circle stands, the ball travels slower and higher, and a gentle step toward the ball is allowed. Walking feet only, because the ball is never worth a collision.

Safety

The ball is the record's whole apparatus and it is soft by law: beach-ball weight, beach-ball air, a ball that stings a palm or startles a face at arm's length is deflated and replaced before the circle sits. Taps are flat-handed and upward, always: a downward spike or a thrown ball is rested in the adult's hands for one flight while the law is re-met kindly, because a ball driven at a seated player's face is the session's one real unpleasantness and it is designed out by the tapping rule, not by hope. Players stay seated and reach from their seats, leaning, yes; lunging across a neighbour, no, and the floor inside the circle is clear, so a landed ball is retrieved at arm's length, not crawled for under chairs. Balls are never sat on, lain on, or stood on between flights: a beach ball under a body is a fall with a soundtrack.

Source: Wikipedia: Beach ball

Inclusion notes

The circle is a court that fits its players: seats can be chairs, cushions, or a bench, spaced so the ball's road reaches everybody, and the adult, who serves each new flight, serves it toward whoever's turn for a visit the air hasn't chosen lately, because inclusion in this record is partly the server's quiet craft. A tap can be made with flat hands, with one hand, with a forearm, or with a foam paddle held in both hands for a player whose reach needs extending. The ball cannot tell the difference and neither can the score, because the count belongs to the circle. A player can hold the session's second honour, the counter's job. Calling the flight's taps aloud at nap-volume commentary, 'three... FOUR...', from any seat, and the final flight's count is always given to two voices together.
