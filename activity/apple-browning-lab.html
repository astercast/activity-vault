Apple Browning Lab

Why do cut apples turn brown? Kids run the experiment and find out.

Ages
7–12
Group
2–30 kids
Time
20–35 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Slice each apple into 4 wedges (adults slice, or hand out pre-cut wedges). Set out cups labeled: plain water, lemon-juice water, salt water, nothing.

Materials

  • apples (one per group)
  • lemon juice
  • water
  • a little salt
  • small cups
  • plates
  • kid-safe knives or pre-sliced apple wedges

Steps

  1. Soak one wedge in each liquid for 2 minutes. Leave one wedge untouched on a plate.
  2. Line all four wedges up and predict: which will stay the freshest-looking after 20 minutes?
  3. Wait. Do another activity or read aloud while the clock runs.
  4. Compare the wedges. The lemon-juice one usually stays pale — the acid slows the browning reaction.
  5. Taste test (optional): does the protected wedge taste different? Discuss what 'browning' actually is — air reacting with the apple's flesh.

Variations

Easier: Test just two treatments: lemon juice vs. nothing.

Harder: Try more treatments (vinegar, honey water, milk) and have kids design the experiment themselves, including a control.

⚠️ Safety

Check for fruit allergies first, and have everyone wash hands before and after handling food.
