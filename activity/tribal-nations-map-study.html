Tribal Nations Map Study

Whose land is the school on? Find the tribal nations of your region on a real map and learn their names right.

Before you begin

Ages
9–14
Group
4–32 kids
Time
30–45 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Get the map from a source made with tribal input, your state tribal-relations office, a tribal nation's own site, or a university Indigenous-studies program all publish them. Find the nations nearest your school yourself before the session, so you can pronounce every name on the map.

Materials

  • a tribal-nations map of your state or region (printed or projected)
  • index cards or cardstock (one per pair)
  • markers

Steps

  1. Start with the honest question: this town sits on land Indigenous peoples have lived on for thousands of years. Which nations call this region home, and are still here?
  2. Find your school's area on the map together. Read the nearest nations' names aloud, carefully, twice. Getting a name right is a form of respect you can practice.
  3. Each pair claims one nation and builds its card: the name the nation uses for itself, where its government sits today, and one fact taken from the nation's own website or book.
  4. Post the cards around the map in roughly the right places. The wall should now show neighbors, not a history exhibit.
  5. Close by naming what changed: these are present-day governments and communities with websites, schools, and courts, not peoples who vanished.

Variations

Easier: Work from the map only: name, location, and one fact read aloud from the card you are given.

Harder: Add a second card per nation: one question the pair still has, and where they would go to find the answer from the nation's own sources.

Inclusion notes

Use maps and facts produced by tribal nations themselves wherever you can, and the names nations use for themselves. This is a study activity, not a ceremony, nobody performs an acknowledgment script unless your school has its own, developed with tribal partners.
