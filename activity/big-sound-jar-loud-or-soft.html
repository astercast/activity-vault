Big Sound Jar, Loud or Soft

Big sealed jars, one shake each: some jars are LOUD and some are SOFT, and the ears of the room file every one on its right mat.

Ages 3–3 · 10–15 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–3
Group
2–10 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Make the jars like an instrument-maker who will be audited by toddlers: fill them in two honest families, loud (two or three big bells, or four wooden beads, in a jar with room to travel) and soft (rice or lentils, a third full, so the sound pours rather than rattles), then seal every lid, tape it round, and shake each jar yourself at arm's length. Any jar whose sound sits between the families, the maybes, is refilled or retired: at three, the record's filing must be honestly fileable, and a jar that argues with the ears teaches arguing, not listening. Line the jars up in a mixed order you have written down (your own answer key, for the final count), set the two mats a body's width apart on the floor, and learn the drum card and the feather card by heart so you can introduce them without a flicker of doubt: drum is LOUD, feather is SOFT.

Materials

  • big sound jars, a filing set of six to eight (big opaque jars with lids the adult has filled, sealed, and tape-locked, the loud ones carrying a few big bells or wooden beads, the soft ones carrying rice or dried lentils; every jar shaken by the adult first, at arm's length, against their own ear)
  • two big filing mats (the LOUD mat and the SOFT mat. Two floor cards the adult has marked big and clear: a drum drawn on one, a feather on the other; the room learns them before the first jar appears)

Steps

  1. Set the two mats where the whole circle can see them, hold up the cards' meaning first ('This drum means LOUD. A sound that fills the room. This feather means SOFT, a sound that lands like this, ' and let a feather of paper fall, if you have one, in the hush) ', and today your ears are the filing clerks.' Say the plain framing: 'These jars are sealed, and they stay sealed, what is inside them is not the lesson, and nobody will open one to check. Inside, some jars carry LOUD sounds and some carry SOFT ones. One shake tells your ears which. Your job: shake once, listen properly, and file the jar on its mat. The jar will not be offended. Jars are professional.' State the laws whole: 'One shake per jar, a good shake, at your chest, never beside anybody's ears, never beside your own. While a jar shakes, the room is quiet: listening is everybody's job, not just the shaker's. A filed jar stays filed until the final count. Ears may argue later, at the count, kindly.'
  2. First filing together, yours: take the first jar, hold it where every eye can see it, and shake it once at full ceremony, one shake, no more, while the room does the listening at full size, which at three looks like hands going still and mouths closing by themselves. Give your own ears their moment in the open ('I hear it landing heavy and bright... my ears say LOUD') and file the jar on the drum mat with the session's official report, said whole: 'Jar one: LOUD. Filed.' Then the record's honesty is established in the open, because it governs everything after: 'If your ears heard it differently, keep your hearing. At the final count we may argue, kindly, with shakes, and the jars will be re-heard by the whole room. Filing is not finished until the count.'
  3. Filing round: shakers take the jars in known order, one each, shake held at the chest, ONE shake, while the room listens in the official hush and the shaker files the jar on the mat their own ears choose, giving the report in the official shape: 'Jar four: SOFT, filed.' A shaker who cannot decide shakes once more, the record allows exactly one appeal shake, announced as such ('An appeal, ONE more'), because listening again is a scientific act, and then files by their best hearing. The adult guards only the constitution: jars shaken at chests, hush kept kindly ('Clerks, the jar is speaking'), and never, not once, a hint from the adult's face about which mat is right, the adult's face in this record is a sealed jar too.
  4. Borderline stretch, for a room whose ears have grown sharp: the last jar in the set is the adult's planted puzzle, a jar filled to sit nearer the border than its brothers (beads in a fuller jar, their rattle muffled), introduced honestly as such before it is shaken: 'This last jar is the difficult file. Grown-up clerks argue about jars like this one. Shake it twice, it has earned two, and file it where your ears truly say, and know that the count may move it, and moving it will be the room learning, not the shaker failing.' The argument at its filing is the lesson's summit and is given its time: two hearings, two opinions stated with their evidence ('It landed heavy, but SMALL'), and a filing by the shaker's own final call.
  5. Finish with the final count, the record's audit, conducted like a ceremony because it is one: the jars on each mat are counted aloud. 'The LOUD mat holds four. The SOFT mat holds three', and then each jar is re-shaken once by its own filer while the whole room confirms or appeals its file with a show of the drum-feather hands (flat palm down for drum, fingers floating down for feather, the room's own signing). A jar the room moves is moved by its filer, with the record's line said over it at full size: 'The room heard it again, and the room knows more than it did. That is not a mistake being fixed. That is ears, working.' The tell-back closes the files: 'Seven jars, shaken once each, filed by ear, loud on the drum, soft on the feather, and one difficult file argued like professionals. Nobody opened a thing. The ears did all of it.' The jars return to their shelf unopened, where their mystery keeps, filed, and faintly audible whenever the shelf is bumped, which the room will notice for weeks.

Variations

Easier: A four-jar version: two loud, two soft, the whole filing office inside ten minutes for the newest clerks.

Harder: A matching version (a room that has filed before): twin jars hide in the set, two jars with the same sound, and after the filing, shakers hunt the pair by ear alone, the mats' work done, the office become a detective agency.

⚠️ Safety

The jars are sealed by the adult, tape-locked, and audited before the session and after it: a lid that turns, cracks, or weeps a single grain retires its jar from the set instantly and permanently. The fillings (bells, beads, rice, lentils) are choking hazards the record keeps behind a sealed lid as its first law, and no jar is ever opened in the room, by anyone, for any verdict; the final count argues with shakes, not with evidence. Shaking happens at the shaker's own chest, one shake at a time: never beside ears, anybody's, including the shaker's, and never thrown, dropped on purpose, or rolled across the floor; a jar is carried in two hands between shakings like the instrument it is. Jars are big, light when dropped by accident, and plastic or well-padded: glass has no place in a filing office run by three-year-olds, and the adult's set uses none.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The filing office honours every kind of ear: a clerk who hears with their hands holds each jar against their own flat palm, or lets a partner shake it while both their hands rest on it, and files by the jar's travel, which the loud jars give generously and the soft ones give as a pour; both are real evidence and the final count treats them so. Filing can be done by direction ('the feather mat, THERE'), by pointing at the drum or feather card the adult holds up one at a time, or by the room's signing at the count, and the appeal shake, the office's great dignity, belongs to any clerk on request, requested in any register. The hush-keeper, the clerk whose held-up hand calls the room to listening before each shake, holds the office's official silence and rotates by list from any seat.
