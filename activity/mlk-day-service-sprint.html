MLK Day Service Sprint

The holiday is a day on, not a day off — pick one real job this building needs and finish it in one session.

Ages
7–14
Group
4–30 kids
Time
30–50 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Scout three jobs in advance that are real, visible, and finishable inside the session: straightening the library shelves, a playground litter sweep, thank-you cards for the custodians and kitchen staff. Ask the custodian what would actually help before you promise anything.

Materials

  • work gloves (for litter jobs)
  • trash bags
  • card-making supplies (paper, markers)

Steps

  1. Say why this holiday, plainly: since 1994, Martin Luther King Jr. Day has also been a national day of service. King's own line is the brief: 'Everybody can be great, because everybody can serve.'
  2. Put the three jobs on the board. The group picks one by show of hands; ties go to the job the building's staff asked for.
  3. Split into crews with one captain each, hand out gloves and bags or card supplies, and set the boundary and the finish line out loud.
  4. Do the work — most of the session is this step. Adults work alongside, same gloves, same bags.
  5. Inspect the finished job together, then close in a circle: whose day got a little easier because of this hour? That answer is the whole point of the holiday.

Variations

Easier: Take the seated job — sorting, card-making, shelf-facing — it is full service, not the soft option.

Harder: Plan the next one: crews draft a one-page proposal for a bigger service job, with the staff member's name on it whose work it supports.

⚠️ Safety

Gloves on for litter, and anything sharp or suspicious is adult-only — point at it, never pick it up. Crews stay inside the agreed boundary where one adult can see them.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Service means work the building actually needs, chosen with the people who do that work every day. Ask first; never perform helping at someone. Every crew's job counts the same when the circle closes.
