Measure the Pumpkin

Circumference, weight, seed guesses — a pumpkin turns into a math lab.

Ages
6–12
Group
2–30 kids
Time
25–45 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Set out one pumpkin per small group with a tape measure and a recording sheet (columns: guess / actual for circumference, weight, seed count).

Materials

  • pumpkins in a few different sizes (one per small group)
  • tape measures
  • a scale (bathroom scale works)
  • paper and pencils for recording

Steps

  1. Each group predicts: how many inches around their pumpkin, how heavy it is, and how many seeds are inside. Write every guess down first — no measuring yet.
  2. Measure the circumference at the widest point with the tape measure. Record the actual number next to the guess.
  3. Weigh the pumpkin. Compare — who guessed closest?
  4. Estimate the seed count. Then the teacher cuts the pumpkin open (adults only on the knife) and each group scoops and counts their pumpkin's seeds in piles of ten.
  5. Compare seed counts: does the biggest pumpkin hold the most seeds? Usually not — great discussion starter.

Variations

Easier: Skip the seed count and just do circumference and weight predictions.

Harder: Graph every group's results and look for patterns between size, weight, and seed count.

⚠️ Safety

Only the adult handles the knife when opening pumpkins. Wash hands after handling seeds and pulp.
