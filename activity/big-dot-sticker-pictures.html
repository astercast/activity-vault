Big Dot Sticker Pictures

Peel a big dot sticker, place it where you want it. The pincer practice that comes before scissors, pens, and everything else.

Ages 3–5 · 15–25 min · 3 materials

Ages
3–5
Group
2–24 kids
Time
15–25 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Bend each sticker sheet back slightly along the rows before handing it out. A sheet that has been loosened peels kindly, and a sheet that hasn't is ten minutes of an adult peeling dots while twenty kids wait. Put out two or three colours only; the whole rainbow turns the session into choosing, not sticking.

Materials

  • large dot stickers (2cm across or bigger, a few sheets per kid)
  • sturdy paper or card (one sheet per kid)
  • crayons (for drawing around the dots, optional)

Steps

  1. Show the peel once, slowly, with your fingertips at the edge of one big dot: pinch, lift, carry it over, press it down flat and pat it. Then do the hard part out loud: 'It stuck to my finger. That happens. Peel it off the finger, stick it on the paper.'
  2. Everyone peels and sticks freely for the first minutes. Dots anywhere, in rows, in piles, in one glorious corner stack. There is no picture to copy and no wrong place for a dot.
  3. When sheets are warming up, offer the first gentle shape: 'Can your dots walk across the page?' A line of dots travelling edge to edge is the first sticker picture most kids make, and it is enough.
  4. Offer crayons for anyone who wants them: draw around the dots, join dots together, give a dot a face. The sticker starts the picture; the kid finishes the sentence.
  5. Close with the flat-pat round: everyone pats every one of their dots down one last time, a dot half-peeled at pickup time is a picture that loses pieces in the backpack, then names go on the back, as dictated, in adult handwriting.

Variations

Easier: The adult pre-peels the first row and leaves the dots hanging half-off the sheet edge, ready to pinch; smaller sheet of paper, fewer dots.

Harder: Stick to a plan: an outline shape to fill with dots (a big circle, a first initial), or a dot pattern, red, blue, red, blue, read off a finished example.

⚠️ Safety

Big dots only at this age, and stickers live on paper, skin, and clothes by agreement, never over eyes, never in mouths, and a dot found heading for a mouth gets swapped for a crayon job, quietly. Sheets and stray dots get collected at the end; a loose sticker on the floor is a slipping spot.
