Egg-and-Spoon Race

A spoon, a wobbling egg, and a finish line that punishes every hurried step.

Before you begin

Ages
4–12
Group
4–30 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Mark a start and a finish on grass or a gym floor, fifteen to twenty big steps apart for young racers and farther for older ones. Use a stand-in unless the group is ready for real shells and the cleanup that follows them. One spoon and one egg per racer, and a spare basket of eggs at the start line.

Materials

  • spoons (one per racer)
  • eggs, or stand-ins: ping pong balls, small potatoes, cotton balls

Steps

  1. Racers line up, spoon held out in one hand at waist height, egg balanced in the spoon's bowl. The free hand goes behind the back.
  2. On 'go', walk fast. Running breaks eggs; the quickest walker with the steadiest hand wins this race.
  3. A dropped egg stops its racer: pick it up, set it back on the spoon where it fell, and only then walk on. No steadying the egg with a thumb; thumbs are famous cheats in this race.
  4. Cross the finish with the egg still riding the spoon. In relay form, pass spoon and egg to the next racer without any hand touching the egg.

Variations

Easier: Balance the egg on a big serving spoon, shorten the course, and let racers walk in pairs who may steady each other by the elbow.

Harder: Add a slalom of cones and a turn at the far end, or race with the spoon in the non-dominant hand.

Safety

Walk, do not run, is a safety rule as much as a game rule: racers watch their spoons, so the course stays clear of holes, hoses, and other racers' lanes. Real eggs mean slippery spills underfoot; stop and wipe them at once, or race with stand-ins.
