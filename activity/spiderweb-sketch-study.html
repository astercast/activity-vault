Spiderweb Sketch Study

Find a real web, sketch it slow, label the spokes: quiet science.

Before you begin

Ages
7–14
Group
2–30 kids
Time
15–30 min
Where
Indoors & Outdoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Scout the area ahead of time for real webs - fence corners, playground equipment, bushes. Morning dew makes them easy to spot.

Materials

  • paper
  • pencils
  • clipboards (optional)
  • magnifiers (optional)

Steps

  1. Find a web and settle in - no touching, just looking.
  2. Sketch what you see: the anchor lines, the spokes, the spiral. Slow is the whole point.
  3. Label the parts: spokes (the straight lines), spiral (the sticky circle), anchor (where it ties off).
  4. Watch for 5 quiet minutes: does the spider move? Does anything land?
  5. Back inside: compare sketches - no two real webs match.

Variations

Easier: Sketch from a printed photo of a web at the table.

Harder: Measure your web's diameter and count the spokes - real field data.

Safety

Look with eyes and magnifiers only - never poke a web or its spider.

Why it works

Slow looking is attention training: close observation and sketching give selective attention a real workout.

Inclusion notes

Photo-based version works for anyone who can't get to the webs; sketching needs no art skill - labels count as success.
