Felt Tree, One by One

A big felt tree low on the wall and big soft pieces to press onto it, one kid, one piece, one at a time — a first tree to decorate.

Ages
3–3
Group
4–16 kids
Time
10–20 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Fix the tree up yourself before the group arrives: flat against a low wall or board, bottom edge at a seated kid's shoulder height, and press every piece onto it yourself once — a piece that slides off in your hand will slide off in theirs, so rough felt or a dot of repositionable glue on the tree fixes it now. Pieces go in the basket beside the tree, all of them, so choosing is part of the activity.

Materials

  • a big felt or flannel tree shape (adult-cut, pinned or taped flat at kid height)
  • big felt stars, circles and simple shapes (adult-cut, each bigger than a kid's palm)
  • a basket (for the pieces)

Steps

  1. Name the real thing in one plain sentence: 'At Christmas, lots of families decorate a tree, putting one thing on at a time until it is full. This is our tree, and we are decorating it our size.' State the one rule: 'One piece at a time. Press it flat with your whole hand.'
  2. First piece together: a kid chooses from the basket, carries it to the tree in two hands, and presses it on with a whole palm while the group counts the press — 'one!' — out loud. The count is the ceremony; a tree decorated one counted piece at a time takes exactly as long as it takes.
  3. Go around the same way, one piece per kid per round, the group counting up after every press: the number the group reaches is the number of pieces on the tree, checked by pointing at each one as it is counted. Counting things you put there yourself is the sturdiest counting there is.
  4. Full-tree moment: when the basket is empty, everyone steps back — or wheels back — and looks. Name what the group can see in plain words: the colours, the star somebody chose for the top, the piece hanging lowest. Looking at finished work together is part of finishing it.
  5. Taking-down is a second activity, not a chore: pieces come off one at a time, counted down by the group, each carried back to the basket by its decorator. The tree ends bare and the basket ends full, ready for the next group to do the whole thing again.

Variations

Easier: A smaller tree and eight pieces, with the adult's hand over theirs for the press — the choice of WHICH piece is still entirely the kid's.

Harder: A pattern round: the adult quietly names a shape — 'the tree needs a star' — and the decorator has to find that shape in the basket before the press.

⚠️ Safety

Pieces are palm-size or bigger and plain felt — no pins, no hooks, no small ornaments, nothing glass, and nothing with a button battery goes anywhere near this tree. The tree is fixed flat by an adult and checked daily; a peeling corner gets re-taped before a kid finds it with their fingers.

🤝 Inclusion notes

The tree's low half belongs to seated decorators: pieces placed from a chair, a lap tray or the floor reach the tree in full, and the counting counts every press the same. A kid who would rather be the basket-keeper — choosing which pieces are offered next — is running the decorating, not watching it.
