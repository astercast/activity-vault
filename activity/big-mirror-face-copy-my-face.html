Big Mirror Face, Copy My Face

Two partners sit face to face. One makes a face — any face at all. The other copies it, like a mirror that is also a friend.

Ages
3–3
Group
4–14 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

There is nothing to fetch and everything to decide: the pairs. Set them before the circle sits — a confident face-maker with a shy one, a still child with a wriggly one — and seat each pair knee to knee at a distance where faces are BIG in each other's view, because at three a face a room away is a rumour. Rehearse your own demonstration face until it is readably one thing — the adult's first face teaches the game's size: not a subtle eyebrow (a rumour) but a whole event — cheeks puffed, eyes wide, mouth a round O — a face that survives being copied by a three-year-old and comes back recognisable. The record is taught by a face, or it is not taught at all.

Materials

  • no materials at all — the group's own faces are the entire equipment, which is the record's first lesson: you brought everything you need

Steps

  1. Sit the pairs down and say the plain framing: 'Your face can do things. Watch mine —' (make the demonstration face, hold it, let it melt) '— that was my Surprised Fish. Now: one of you makes a face, any face, and your partner copies it, like a mirror. Mirrors in this room are kind: they copy what they see as best they can, and a copy that comes out different is not wrong — it is a NEW face, and new faces get copied back.' State the laws whole: 'Faces are made of faces only — no touching anybody's face, ever. A face is held while your partner looks: two whole breaths. Either partner can say 'new face' and the making passes over, no reason needed.'
  2. First copying together, yours: take a partner from the circle, make the Surprised Fish at full ceremony, hold it for two breaths while your partner studies it like weather, and let them build their copy piece by piece in the open — 'Cheeks out... eyes BIG... mouth like an O' — naming the pieces as coaching, never as correction, until the pair of you sit face to face, two fish, regarding each other gravely. Hold the moment one breath longer than feels necessary, then melt together, and say the record's line over the melt: 'Your face knew mine. That is what faces are FOR, it turns out.'
  3. Copying round: pairs take the making in turns round the circle — the maker composes a face (the record's gallery grows in the open: Sleepy Bear, with heavy lids and a yawn held hostage; Pop-Eyes; the Long Face, chin down, everything mournful; the Cheek-Puff Champion) — holds it two breaths, and the mirror builds the copy while the room, watching each pair in turn, witnesses without scoring: the only verdict the room gives is recognition, called out when it lands: 'THERE it is — two Sleepy Bears!' A copy that wanders into its own invention is received under the record's constitution: 'Your mirror made a new face. Maker — copy THAT one back.' And the round becomes a conversation faces have with each other, which is what it always was.
  4. Fast-mirror stretch, offered to the pairs whose copying has grown certain: the maker changes faces slowly — fish, bear, pop-eyes — with a breath between each, and the mirror follows a half-breath behind, like a pond catching up. Nobody speeds past the following: the stretch is not quickness, it is TOGETHERNESS, and the record names it while it happens: 'You are not behind. A mirror is always a moment behind — that is how you know it is a mirror and not a photograph.' Pairs that wobble into giggling have not failed the stretch; the giggle is a face, and it will be copied too.
  5. Finish with the whole-circle mirror: one maker — the day's steadiest — stands where every face can see them, makes the group's final face (chosen by acclamation from the day's gallery), and the WHOLE circle builds it at once: twenty Surprised Fish, melting together on the maker's melt, the room regarding itself. Faces rest, and the tell-back is said plainly: 'Every face in this room was looked at properly today, and every face was copied by a friend. Being copied is what being known feels like at three. It feels like that later too — it just gets quieter.'

Variations

Easier: A one-pair version: a single pair, four faces, the full ceremony inside ten minutes for the newest groups or a single new friendship being built on purpose.

Harder: A family-faces version: the gallery is built from home — a face somebody's grandmother makes, the dog's face, a baby sibling's face — each made, copied, and honoured with its story in one sentence.

⚠️ Safety

Faces are made of faces only: no hands on anybody's face, no pulled cheeks or held eyelids — a face that needs hands to make it is retired and replaced, kindly, because the game's whole dignity is that the copying partner must be able to copy safely too. Pairs sit knee to knee and stay seated: leaning in is done by faces growing in view, not bodies tipping, and a pair that migrates closer and closer in its enthusiasm is re-seated once, kindly, at mirror distance. Any child may take the whole session as the room's gallery-keeper — watching pairs, calling the recognitions ('THERE it is!') — at full honour; a face that wants to be watched by the room but not copied by one partner is accommodated by the keeper's role, not negotiated away. Two breaths is the holding limit the adult keeps: a face held to the point of aching cheeks is melted by the adult's own melt, in solidarity, on sight.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Copying works across every distance the room contains: a mirror can copy from a seat beside the maker rather than opposite (some faces read better from the side), a face can be made by direction — 'cheeks OUT... eyes WIDE' — with a partner's face following the words while the composition stays the director's, and a copy can be answered in any register the copyist owns, including a described copy ('I am doing the fish — my mouth is the O') which the maker accepts or tops up, in words. The gallery-keeper's job — the room's recognition called out loud — is one of the session's two honours and belongs to whoever holds it, and the final face's maker is chosen by the circle's acclamation, never by the adult's favour.
