Big Shadow Grow and Shrink

One big cardboard shape, one lamp, one wall: move the shape nearer the light and its shadow grows — the group directs, the shadow obeys.

Ages
3–3
Group
3–12 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
Some prep needed

Setup

Set the stage before the group sits: lamp steady and adult-height-handled, wall clear, shape tested through its whole journey — from right against the wall to right against the light — so you know the shadow stays on the wall and the shape never has to touch anything hot or bright. The shape is plain and bold: a three-year-old reads a shadow by its outline, and a shape with ears, points or a tail gives the group something to watch change size. Nobody carries the lamp but you; that is fixed before the first shadow appears.

Materials

  • one big cardboard shape (adult-cut: a star, a fish, a hand — bigger than two kid hands, held on a stick or by its edge)
  • one lamp or bright torch on a stable stand (adult-positioned, shining at a clear wall)
  • a room that can be dimmed a little

Steps

  1. Darken the room a little, switch the lamp on, and make the first shadow yourself without a word of explanation: shape against the wall, a small neat shadow — then walk it slowly toward the light and let the group watch the shadow swell across the wall. Their reaction IS the introduction. Then say the plain sentence it all hangs on: 'The shadow does what the shape does — but bigger, when the shape goes nearer the light.'
  2. State the one rule: 'Only the grown-up holds the light and the shape. Everybody else is a director — you tell it: nearer... further... stop.' Directors' hands stay in laps or pointing; pointing is a director's whole toolkit and it is enough.
  3. Directing round: the group directs the shape's journey in chorus and by turns — 'NEARER!' as it creeps toward the lamp and the shadow climbs the wall, 'further...' as it shrinks back down. Call the sizes as they pass: 'As big as the door! As big as a cat!' — naming a shadow's size against real things is measurement in the only units this room needs.
  4. Freeze moments: on a director's 'STOP!', the shape freezes and everyone looks hard at the shadow's edges — fuzzy when the shape is near the light, sharper when it is near the wall. Say only what eyes can check: 'Near the light, the shadow is big and soft at the edges. Near the wall, small and sharp.' Two facts, both visible, both theirs now.
  5. Finish with the biggest possible shadow: the group directs the shape as near the light as it safely goes, the shadow fills the wall, and every director puts their own hands up into the light's edge from their seats to see their hand-shadows join it — a wall full of shadow, made by the whole room, before the lamp goes off and the ordinary wall comes back.

Variations

Easier: Two positions only, marked on the floor: BIG (near the light) and SMALL (near the wall) — directors call just the two words and the shape travels between them.

Harder: A second shape joins: the group directs two shadows to the same size — one near, one far, matched by eye — and argues about whether they truly match, which is the point.

⚠️ Safety

The lamp is the adult's, positioned once and never carried, aimed at a wall and never at eyes — its brightness is respected the way this archive respects every bright thing, by looking at what it lights, not at it. The shape travels in the adult's hands only, so no small body ever crosses between a hot or bright lamp and the wall. Directors direct from their seats; the floor between seats and wall stays empty, because a shadow stage with a toddler wandering it teaches collisions, not optics.

🤝 Inclusion notes

Directing is the activity, and it runs on any signal a director has: a word, a point toward or away from the light, a big-arms gesture for GROW and hands-close for shrink — every director's call moves the shape the same distance. A director can also hold the SECOND shape at the wall end, still, as the size the moving shadow must match — the fixed point the whole experiment leans on, held by its most patient scientist.
