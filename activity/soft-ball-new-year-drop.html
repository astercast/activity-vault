Soft Ball New Year Drop

Count five down to one on your fingers, and the grown-up drops one big soft ball into the basket. That is the whole New Year party at three.

Ages 3–5 · 10–15 min · 2 materials

Ages
3–5
Group
4–20 kids
Time
10–15 min
Where
Indoors
Prep
✅ No prep needed

Setup

Put the basket in the middle of the circle and test the drop yourself first: standing, ball held high, dropped straight down. It should land in the basket and stay there. If it bounces out, use a softer ball or a deeper basket; a runaway ball ends the count, not starts a chase.

Materials

  • one big soft ball (beach-ball size or a soft playground ball)
  • a wide basket, laundry basket, or big open box

Steps

  1. Teach the count on fingers first, no ball: hold up five fingers, fold one down at a time, five, four, three, two, one, and everybody shouts the last one together. Practise twice; the shout is the payoff the count is building to.
  2. Now the drop. You hold the ball high over the basket. The group counts down on fingers, and on 'one', not before, the ball drops. The rule the kids police for you: the ball only falls on one.
  3. Round two, a kid is the dropper, standing on a taped spot beside the basket with the ball held high in two hands. Dropping is a two-hand job at three; nobody throws, nobody aims from across the room.
  4. Make the wait longer, never the count: hold at 'two' for a long silly pause while the room holds its breath, then fold the last finger. The pause is where the laughing lives.
  5. Finish with the group drop: everyone counts, the dropper drops, and the whole circle does the cheer with both arms up. Then the ball goes in the basket and stays there while you say plainly that the party game is done.

Variations

Easier: Count down from three instead of five, and the adult drops every round. A shorter wait is still a real drop.

Harder: Two baskets and two droppers, counting together: both balls have to land on the same 'one', which takes three or four honest tries.

⚠️ Safety

Soft ball only, dropped straight down from a standing hold, never thrown, never dropped off furniture, and nobody stands or sits in the basket. The dropper stands on the taped spot; reaching into the basket waits until the ball has stopped moving.
